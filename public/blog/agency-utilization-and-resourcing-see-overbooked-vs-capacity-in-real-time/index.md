# Agency Utilization and Resourcing: See Overbooked vs Capacity in Real Time

URL: https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/agency-utilization-and-resourcing-see-overbooked-vs-capacity-in-real-time/
Published: 2026-11-03
Summary: How agencies can track utilization and plan resources: define targets, connect time tracking to billing, forecast capacity and spot overbooked people early.

**In short:** Utilization is the share of a person's available time spent on work that is billable or otherwise planned. Resourcing is deciding who works on what, and when. Agencies that track both in real time can see who is overbooked, who has spare capacity, which projects are running over and whether the pipeline can be staffed, before problems reach the client. Without it, decisions are made on feel and discovered late.

## Key Takeaways

- Define **utilization precisely:** what counts as available time, billable time and non-billable time.
- Set **targets by role,** since a director and a designer should not share one number.
- **Time tracking must be easy and approved,** or the data will be wrong.
- Connect **hours to projects, budgets and invoices,** so you can see margin as the project runs.
- Forecast **forward capacity** from planned allocations and the sales pipeline, not only past hours.
- Use the numbers to **balance workloads,** not to police individuals.

## Key Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Available hours | Working time minus leave, holidays and fixed non-project time |
| Billable hours | Time on client work that is charged |
| Utilization rate | Billable hours divided by available hours |
| Planned allocation | Hours assigned to projects for future dates |
| Capacity | Available hours not yet allocated |
| Realization | Value billed compared with value of time recorded |

Agree these definitions in writing. Different departments using different formulas is a common cause of confusion.

## Step 1: Set Targets That Make Sense

Targets should reflect the role and the business model. Senior roles carry business development and management time. Delivery roles may carry higher billable shares. Set them with the team, review them quarterly and compare against what the data shows is realistic.

## Step 2: Make Time Tracking Reliable

- **Log daily,** against projects and tasks, in the tool people already use.
- **Keep categories few and clear.** Too many causes guesswork.
- **Require approval** by project leads before time flows to billing.
- **Flag gaps,** such as days with no entries.
- **Lock periods** once invoiced, with a change process.

Approval is not about distrust. It gives clients accurate invoices and gives you clean data.

## Step 3: Plan Resources Forward

Look at the next six to twelve weeks:

1. List **committed projects** with their planned hours by role.
2. Add **likely deals** from the pipeline, weighted by probability.
3. Compare with **available capacity** by role and week.
4. Identify **gaps and overloads,** then decide whether to hire, subcontract, move dates or decline work.

A weekly 30-minute resourcing meeting using one shared view is more effective than ad hoc messages.

## Step 4: Watch for Early Warning Signs

| Signal | What it suggests |
|---|---|
| A person allocated above capacity for several weeks | Burnout risk and slipped deadlines |
| Project burn faster than progress | Under-scoped or scope creep |
| Low utilization across a team | Pipeline gap or poor allocation |
| High utilization with low realization | Write-offs or inefficiency |
| Many unapproved timesheets | Billing delays |

## Link Resourcing to Profit

Utilization alone can mislead. A fully utilised team on under-priced projects is still unprofitable. Add:

- **Project margin:** revenue less cost of time.
- **Budget versus actual** by phase.
- **Rate realization** by client and service.

These show where to raise prices, change scope or stop work that does not pay.

## FAQ

**What is agency utilization?**
Utilization is the share of a person's available time spent on billable or planned work, usually billable hours divided by available hours. Agree on how available time is defined.

**What is a good utilization rate for an agency?**
It depends on role and business model. Senior roles carry management and business development time, so targets differ. Set targets by role and check them against margin, not utilization alone.

**How do you forecast agency capacity?**
Combine planned allocations on committed projects with weighted likely deals from the pipeline, and compare them with available hours by role and week.

**Why require approval of timesheets?**
Approval gives clients accurate invoices and gives you clean data for utilization, margin and estimates.
