# Why AI Matters for Nepal: What the Evidence Shows

URL: https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/why-ai-matters-for-nepal-what-the-evidence-shows/
Published: 2026-10-05
Summary: Nepal leans on remittances and a fast-growing IT export sector. What the data says about why AI matters, who can use it, and where the gaps are.

**In short:** AI matters for Nepal because the economy leans on two things AI touches directly: remittances, which were about 28.6% of GDP in FY 2024-25, and a growing IT services sector that industry bodies put near $1 billion in exports. The government approved a National AI Policy in August 2025, but 44% of people are still offline, and the evidence on how AI is actually being used inside Nepal is thin. The honest reading is a real opportunity with unproven delivery.

## Key Takeaways

- Remittances were about 28.6% of Nepal's GDP in FY 2024-25, against a world average of 5.13%, according to Nepal News citing Nepal Rastra Bank and World Bank data.
- Nepal's IT sector is estimated at about $1 billion in exports in 2025 by an industry association, but there is no official full count.
- The cabinet approved the National AI Policy, 2025 in August 2025. It sets up a National AI Centre and an AI Regulation Council, and critics say implementation is the test.
- DataReportal counts 16.6 million internet users, 56.0% of the population, so about 13.0 million people are offline.
- Nepal ranked 150th in Oxford Insights' 2024 Government AI Readiness Index.

## The Evidence

This piece pulls together five public sources. None of them is an AI study of Nepal, so the argument below is built from the economy and the infrastructure, and we say so where it is our own reasoning.

- **World Bank, April 8, 2026.** In its [Nepal growth outlook](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/04/08/nepal-s-growth-projected-to-moderate-in-fy26-amid-middle-east-conflict-and-lingering-effects-of-domestic-disruptions), the World Bank projects growth of 2.3% in FY26, down from 4.6% in FY25, and an average of 4.4% in FY27-FY28. It names tourism, the IT sector and agribusiness as priority sectors and says private-sector-led growth is critical for creating jobs.
- **Nepal News, April 15, 2026.** A [long read on remittances](https://english.nepalnews.com/s/long-reads/206401/) citing Nepal Rastra Bank and the World Bank reports Rs 1.72 trillion (about $11 billion) in remittances in FY 2024-25, or 28.6% of GDP.
- **Kathmandu Post, February 25, 2026.** An article on [Nepal's IT exports](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/02/25/nepal-s-it-exports-near-1-billion-can-the-momentum-be-sustained) reports the industry estimate of about $1 billion in 2025.
- **Kathmandu Post, August 16, 2025.** Coverage of the [National AI Policy](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2025/08/16/nepal-rolls-out-ambitious-ai-policy).
- **DataReportal, Digital 2026: Nepal.** The [Nepal report](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-nepal) uses data from October 2025.

## What AI Is Doing in Nepal's Economy

We did not find a reliable count of how many Nepali businesses or people use AI tools, and the sources above do not measure it. What the evidence does show is where AI meets the economy.

**Remittances and labour exports.** Remittances were Rs 1.72 trillion in FY 2024-25, 28.6% of GDP, about 2,300 workers leave daily, and the Gulf region supplies 40 to 42% of inflows, according to the Nepal News piece. The World Bank notes that a prolonged Middle East conflict could reduce remittance inflows. Our reasoning, not a finding from these sources: an economy this exposed to one source of income has a strong reason to build other ones, and digital services is the one the World Bank itself names.

**IT services and outsourcing.** The Kathmandu Post reports that the sector's industry body, NAS-IT, estimates about $1 billion in exports in 2025, that employment grew from about 70,000 in 2022 to about 100,000 in 2026, and that markets include Australian home-loan data processing, US healthcare data analytics, AI services and software development. It also reports that there is no central mechanism tracking full IT export earnings, and that the last comprehensive survey, IIDS in 2022, put the figure near $515 million. Our reasoning: much outsourced data and software work is the kind of work AI tools can speed up or automate, so AI is both an opening and a competitive pressure for this sector.

**Government.** The Kathmandu Post reports that the cabinet approved the National AI Policy, 2025, drafted by the Ministry of Communication and Information Technology. It creates a National AI Centre and an AI Regulation Council, and covers ethical and inclusive use, skills, research, data protection and infrastructure such as 5G, optical fibre and data centres. The article does not give funding amounts or quantified targets.

## What Changed

Three things changed recently. First, a national AI policy now exists, approved in August 2025. Second, the IT sector reportedly doubled its exports in three years, from 2022 to 2025, per the same Kathmandu Post article. Third, the economy has slowed: the World Bank projects growth of 2.3% in FY26, after 4.6% in FY25, which raises the stakes for finding productivity gains.

What has not changed much is readiness. The Nepal Economic Forum, writing on February 3, 2026, notes that Nepal ranked 150th in Oxford Insights' Government AI Readiness Index 2024, still lacks a comprehensive data protection law, and has limited AI-specific guidelines. We did not verify Nepal's rank in later editions of the index.

## Who Benefits and Who Is Left Out

**Likely beneficiaries.** The sources point to IT services firms and the tourism and agribusiness sectors the World Bank names as priorities. For agriculture specifically, see our post on [satellite data and AI for farmers](/blog/satellite-data-ai-farmers-see-crop-risks-earlier/), which found a peer-reviewed rice-yield study from Nepal's Terai. For education, our review of [AI tutoring trials](/blog/ai-personalized-learning-what-tutoring-trials-show/) shows gains depend on how the tutor is designed.

**Who may be left out.** DataReportal's October 2025 data counts 29.6 million people, 16.6 million internet users (56.0%) and 13.0 million people (44.0%) offline. Mobile connections total 32.4 million, 109% of the population, so connection counts overstate how many people are actually online. Median fixed download speed was 79.79 Mbps. The Kathmandu Post also reports that about 90% of IT employment is in Kathmandu and that AI curriculum is absent from early grades. For the global picture of this kind of gap, see [what usage data shows about AI adoption](/blog/ai-everyday-life-what-the-usage-data-shows/).

## Limitations and Open Questions

- **No Nepal-specific AI usage data.** We found no reliable survey of AI use by Nepali people or firms, so claims that AI is "transforming" Nepal are not backed by the sources here.
- **IT export numbers are disputed.** The $1 billion figure is an industry estimate with no official full count behind it, and payments labelled as remittances can hide export income.
- **Remittance figures vary by source and period.** Search results carried several different shares of GDP for different periods. We used one sourced figure for FY 2024-25 and did not mix them.
- **Policy is not delivery.** A Computer Association of Nepal official told the Kathmandu Post that implementation is crucial and that the policy may not have the desired impact unless the private sector is involved. Funding amounts are not stated.
- **Our own inferences are marked.** The links between remittance dependence, outsourcing and AI are our reasoning, not findings.

## What Happens Next

This section is expectation, not fact. The signals to watch are whether the National AI Centre and the AI Regulation Council are staffed and funded, whether a data protection law passes, whether official IT export statistics improve, and whether connectivity gaps narrow. For businesses in Nepal, the practical reading is to treat AI as a tool to adopt carefully, keep a person accountable for outcomes, and design for customers who may have slow connections or limited experience with AI. ## The Short Version

AI matters for Nepal because the economy depends on remittances and on an IT services sector that AI both helps and competes with. The government has a policy, but readiness, connectivity and skills are the open questions, and there is little hard data yet on how AI is actually being used. Read every figure with its source and period, because the IT export and remittance numbers are estimates.

## FAQ

**Why does AI matter for Nepal?**
Nepal's economy depends heavily on remittances, about 28.6% of GDP in FY 2024-25 against a world average of 5.13%, and on a fast-growing IT services sector that industry bodies estimate at about $1 billion in exports in 2025. AI affects both: it can raise productivity in services, and it can also automate some of the outsourced work that sector sells.

**Does Nepal have a national AI policy?**
Yes. Nepal's cabinet approved the National AI Policy, 2025 in August 2025. According to the Kathmandu Post it creates a National AI Centre under the communication ministry and an AI Regulation Council, and covers skills, research, data protection and infrastructure. A Computer Association of Nepal official said implementation is crucial.

**How many people in Nepal are online?**
DataReportal's Digital 2026 report, using data from October 2025, counts 16.6 million internet users in Nepal, 56.0% of the population, which leaves about 13.0 million people (44.0%) offline. It also counts 32.4 million mobile connections, 109% of the population, because many people hold more than one.

**How big is Nepal's IT export sector?**
There is no official full count. The Nepal Association of Software and Information Technology Companies (NAS-IT) estimates about $1 billion in 2025, while the last comprehensive survey cited by the Kathmandu Post, the Institute for Integrated Development Studies' 2022 survey, put it at about $515 million.
