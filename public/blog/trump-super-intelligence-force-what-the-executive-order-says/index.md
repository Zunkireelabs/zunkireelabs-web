# Trump's Super Intelligence Force: What the Order Says

URL: https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/trump-super-intelligence-force-what-the-executive-order-says/
Published: 2026-10-05
Summary: The US renamed AI 'Super Intelligence' and created a task force. What the order and pledge say, what is unclear, and what it means for businesses.

**In short:** A US executive order renames "Artificial Intelligence" to "Super Intelligence" across the federal government, and a new Super Intelligence Force has 120 days to report on risks and opportunities. It is a change of terminology and organisation. It does not mean that superintelligence, as researchers define it, has been built.

## Key Takeaways

- An executive order reported on September 29, 2026 renames "Artificial Intelligence" to "Super Intelligence" (SI) inside the federal government.
- The Super Intelligence Force, announced on October 4, is chaired by National Intelligence Director Jay Clayton and has 120 days to deliver a report.
- Tech executives signed a safety pledge at the White House. Trump called it "morally binding", but it is not legally enforceable.
- Many details, including the full list of signatories and the commitments themselves, have not been made public.


## What Did the Executive Order Say?

According to [Fox News's live coverage](https://www.foxnews.com/live-news/ai-leaders-trump-meeting-google-executive-order), President Trump signed an executive order on September 29, 2026 that renames "Artificial Intelligence" to "Super Intelligence" (SI) across the executive branch. The order states: "The terminology used by the Federal Government should reflect the transformative capabilities of these technologies."

The order also gives White House officials 60 days to draft proposed legislative language that would define "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in federal law.

## What Is the Super Intelligence Force?

On October 4, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/04/trump-unveils-his-new-super-intelligence-force/) that Trump had created a "Super Intelligence Force" task force to coordinate federal work on advanced AI. As reported:

- **Chair:** Jay Clayton, the National Intelligence Director.
- **Vice chairs:** Andrew Ferguson (FTC Chair), Emil Michael (Undersecretary of War for Research and Engineering) and Scott Kupor (Director of the Office of Personnel Management).
- **Deadline:** 120 days to produce a report on the risks and opportunities of AI and "Super Intelligence".
- **Charter:** it emphasises preventing "overregulation and regulatory capture that would stifle innovation".

Trump said the force will "ensure that America continues to lead the World in Super Intelligence" and "protect the interests, and improve the lives, of all Americans."

## What About the Safety Pledge?

Executives from major AI companies gathered at the White House and signed a safety agreement. Fox News reports that Trump described it as "morally binding", which is different from legally enforceable. TechCrunch calls it a non-binding pledge and says its details remain vague.

The pledge has also drawn criticism. Senator Richard Blumenthal said that violations do not have to be disclosed publicly and that companies can withdraw without legal consequences. Executives from Anthropic, Meta and OpenAI were among the named attendees, but the full list of signatories and the text of the commitments have not been published in the coverage we reviewed.

## Does This Mean Superintelligence Has Arrived?

No. This is a naming and organisational decision, not a technical milestone. In our explainer on [what superintelligence is and why it is called that](/blog/what-is-superintelligence-and-why-is-it-called-that/), we describe superintelligence as a hypothetical AI far beyond human ability across nearly every area. No system is generally agreed to have reached that level, and a government changing its terminology does not change that.

It does show that "superintelligence" is moving from research papers into policy vocabulary, which is why more people are searching for it.

## What Does This Mean for a Business?

Most businesses are not directly affected today, but the direction is worth watching:

1. **Expect new definitions.** The legislative language due in about 60 days could shape how laws describe advanced AI.
2. **Watch the 120-day report.** It may influence future federal guidance on AI risk and regulation.
3. **Do not rely on voluntary pledges.** A pledge you cannot enforce is not a substitute for your own AI governance: named owners, access limits and logs.
4. **Keep terminology precise.** In contracts and marketing, "AI" and "superintelligence" are not interchangeable. Avoid claiming either more than you can support.
5. **Check updates before acting.** Details are still emerging, so confirm against primary sources before changing policy.

## The Short Version

The US government has renamed AI "Super Intelligence" in federal usage, created a task force with a 120-day deadline, and secured a non-binding safety pledge from tech leaders. The facts that matter most, such as the pledge's contents and any future rules, are still unknown. For businesses, the practical response is to keep their own AI oversight in order and follow the report when it arrives.

## FAQ

**What is the Super Intelligence Force?**
It is a US federal task force announced on October 4, 2026, chaired by National Intelligence Director Jay Clayton, with 120 days to report on the risks and opportunities of advanced AI.

**Did Trump rename AI to Super Intelligence?**
Yes, within the federal government. An executive order reported on September 29, 2026 renames "Artificial Intelligence" to "Super Intelligence" (SI) across the executive branch and asks officials to draft legislative language defining the terms within 60 days.

**Is the AI safety pledge legally binding?**
No. Trump described it as "morally binding", but it is not legally enforceable, and critics such as Senator Blumenthal note that companies can withdraw without legal consequences.

**Does this mean superintelligence has been achieved?**
No. The change is about terminology and organisation. No system is generally agreed to have reached superintelligence.
