# Agency Sales-to-Delivery Handoff: Keep Deals and Projects in One System

URL: https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/agency-sales-to-delivery-handoff-keep-deals-and-projects-in-one-system/
Published: 2026-11-03
Summary: How agencies can stop losing detail between sales and delivery: connect the deal pipeline to project setup, scope, milestones and billing, with a checklist.

**In short:** Agencies usually lose money and goodwill in the gap between a signed deal and the start of delivery. What the client was promised sits in an email thread or a salesperson's head, while the delivery team works from a thinner brief. Connecting the deal pipeline to project setup in one system means the scope, price, assumptions and dates travel with the work, so the team starts from what was sold.

## Key Takeaways

- The costliest agency problems often begin at **handoff,** when sold scope and delivered scope drift apart.
- A **single record** from first contact to signed contract to project removes retyping and lost detail.
- Capture **scope, assumptions, exclusions, dates and price structure** at the point of sale, not afterwards.
- Run a **structured handoff** with sales, delivery lead and finance in the same room or thread.
- Link **time tracking and billing** to the project so what was sold can be compared with what is being spent.
- Report on **margin by project,** not just revenue won.

## Where Handoffs Break

| Symptom | Underlying cause |
|---|---|
| Delivery asks the client questions already answered in the sales process | Notes live in email or a personal CRM |
| Scope creep with no change record | Exclusions were never written down |
| Surprise at project start | The delivery team did not see the proposal |
| Under-priced projects | Sales used rough estimates not tied to capacity |
| Invoicing disputes | Billing terms were not carried into project setup |
| Repeated mistakes | No feedback loop from delivery to sales |

## Design the Pipeline Around Delivery

Stages should reflect real decisions and carry data forward:

1. **Lead and qualification:** need, budget, timeline, decision-makers.
2. **Discovery:** requirements and constraints captured in structured fields.
3. **Proposal:** scope, deliverables, exclusions, assumptions, price model and timeline.
4. **Negotiation:** changes tracked against the proposal version.
5. **Signed:** triggers project creation.
6. **Handoff:** structured review, then delivery starts.

At **signed,** the system should create the project with the proposal's deliverables as milestones, the agreed dates, the billing schedule and the client contacts, so nobody retypes them.

## The Handoff Checklist

Before delivery begins, confirm in writing:

- Client goals and what success looks like.
- Deliverables and what is out of scope.
- Assumptions, such as client responsibilities and approval times.
- Dependencies, access and materials needed.
- Timeline, milestones and key dates.
- Commercial terms: fixed price, time and materials or retainer, and how changes are priced.
- Team: who is on the project and their expected hours.
- Risks and sensitivities, including relationship history.
- The first week's plan and the client's first meeting.

A 30-minute handoff meeting using this list prevents weeks of rework.

## Keep a Change Trail

Scope changes are normal. Problems come from unrecorded ones. Use a **change request log** attached to the project: what changed, who asked, effect on time and cost, and who approved it. Delivery leads can then say "yes, and here is the impact" instead of absorbing the work.

After delivery, compare **estimated and actual** hours and cost. Feed this back into proposals, so pricing improves and sales learn which promises are costly. Our post on [AI for agencies](/blog/ai-for-agencies-operating-system-pipeline-proposals-delivery/) takes the operating system view.

## FAQ

**What is a sales-to-delivery handoff in an agency?**
It is the transfer of a signed deal to the team that will deliver it, including scope, exclusions, assumptions, dates, price terms and client context, so delivery starts from what was sold.

**Why do agencies lose margin after a deal is signed?**
Often because sold scope and delivered scope drift apart, changes are not recorded and estimates were not tied to real capacity. A structured handoff and change log help control this.

**What should be in an agency handoff checklist?**
Client goals, deliverables and exclusions, assumptions, dependencies, timeline and milestones, commercial terms, team and hours, risks and the first week's plan.

**Do we need one system for sales and delivery?**
One connected system avoids retyping and lost detail. If you use separate tools, integrate them so the proposal data creates the project automatically.
