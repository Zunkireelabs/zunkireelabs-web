# Where AI Answers Get Their Sources, and Why Listings Matter as Much as Your Site

URL: https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/where-ai-answers-get-their-sources/
Published: 2026-10-13
Summary: Yext studied 17.2 million AI citations. Brand-owned sites supplied 46.89% and managed third-party listings 43.71%. What that means for AI visibility.

**In short:** Most businesses that want to appear in ChatGPT or Perplexity put all their effort into their own website. A study of 17.2 million AI citations published by Yext in September 2026 suggests that is roughly half the job: brand-owned websites supplied 46.89% of citations, and managed third-party listings supplied 43.71%. For ChatGPT in particular, listings outweighed the brand's own site. The figures come from a vendor that sells listing management, so treat the exact percentages as directional, but the direction is consistent across several independent studies.

## Key Takeaways

- Yext Research's "AI Citation Behavior Across Models (Q4 2025)", reported by Lauryn Chamberlain on 22 September 2026, analysed 17.2 million distinct AI citations across four models and seven sectors.
- Across all models, brand-owned websites accounted for 46.89% of citations and managed third-party listings for 43.71%. Reviews and social added 5.48%, and news, forums and other sources 3.92%.
- The split varies sharply by model. For ChatGPT, listings were the larger source at 53.36% against 39.55% for brand-owned sites. For Gemini the order reversed, at 51.62% site against 39.15% listings.
- Ahrefs has reported that only about 12% of URLs cited by AI tools overlap with Google's top-10 organic results, so ranking well in Google does not guarantee being cited in an AI answer.
- Every figure here comes from a company that sells search or listing tools. The pattern is corroborated across studies; the precise numbers are not independently audited.

## What the Study Actually Measured

Yext grouped the sources behind AI answers into four buckets: the brand's own website, managed third-party listings such as directories and profile pages, reviews and social posts, and everything else including news and forums.

Combined, the first two buckets accounted for about 90.6% of citations. That is the useful finding. The overwhelming majority of what an AI model says about a company comes from surfaces the company can either publish or claim, rather than from press coverage it cannot control.

Two further details are worth noting. Listings made up 54.53% of the distinct cited URLs, meaning they contributed more unique pages. Brand websites, however, averaged 4.31 citation occurrences per URL against 2.46 for listings, meaning each page on your own site tended to be cited more often once it was cited at all.

## Why the Model You Care About Changes the Answer

The per-model breakdown is the part most businesses miss.

| Model | Brand-owned site | Managed listings |
|---|---|---|
| Gemini | 51.62% | 39.15% |
| Perplexity | 46.11% | 43.41% |
| Claude | 41.27% | 45.93% |
| ChatGPT | 39.55% | 53.36% |

If your buyers ask ChatGPT, your directory and profile pages were the larger source in this dataset. If they ask Gemini, your own website was. A strategy built only around your website optimises for one end of that range.

This fits a related finding from other 2026 analyses: the overlap between the domains ChatGPT cites and those Perplexity cites is small, reported at around 11% in one audit of a large citation sample. Different assistants are reading substantially different parts of the web.

## Vendor Claim or Independent Evidence?

It matters who produced these numbers.

- **Vendor-produced.** The Yext study comes from a company whose product is listing management, and the conclusion happens to support buying listing management. The 11% overlap audit and most "AI visibility" statistics circulating in 2026 come from GEO and tracking tool vendors.
- **Independently useful.** The studies use different samples and methods yet keep reaching the same qualitative conclusion: AI assistants cite a different mix of sources than Google ranks, and structured profile pages carry real weight.
- **Not established.** Any specific percentage. Sample windows differ, "citation" is defined differently across studies, and none of these datasets has been independently replicated.

Treat the percentages as a prompt to check your own position rather than as a benchmark to hit.

## What to Do About It

1. **Claim and correct your profiles.** LinkedIn, Clutch, GoodFirms, Trustpilot and the directories specific to your industry. Make the description, services and location identical to your website's wording, because inconsistency gives a model competing versions of who you are.
2. **Put the plain definition on your own site.** State what your company is, who it serves and what it does in one self-contained sentence near the top of the page. Assistants lift passages that make sense out of context.
3. **Add Organization structured data with `sameAs`.** List every profile you control. This is the machine-readable link between your website and your listings.
4. **Check what the assistants currently say.** Ask ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude and Google AI Mode the questions your buyers ask, and record which sources they cite. That audit is more useful than any published average.
5. **Watch for pages that describe competitors.** A neutral directory-style page on your own site can be used as the source for a rival's description. If you publish comparisons, keep the detail on your own offer.

## The Short Version

Appearing in AI answers is not only a website problem. In the largest published sample so far, roughly 90% of citations came from surfaces a brand can manage, and nearly half of those were third-party listings rather than the brand's own pages. The figures are vendor-produced and should be treated as directional, but the practical implication is cheap to act on: make your profiles consistent, state plainly on your own site what you do, and connect the two with structured data.
