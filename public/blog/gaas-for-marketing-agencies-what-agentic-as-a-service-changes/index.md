# GaaS for Marketing Agencies: What Agentic as a Service Changes

URL: https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/gaas-for-marketing-agencies-what-agentic-as-a-service-changes/
Published: 2026-10-12
Summary: What does GaaS mean for a marketing agency? How agentic as a service differs from SaaS tools, which agency tasks suit it, and how to test it before you rely on it.

**In short:** For a marketing agency, GaaS (Agentic as a Service) means buying completed tasks done by AI agents instead of buying software seats your team operates. It suits repeatable work such as reporting, research and first drafts. It is still your agency's job to keep quality, client trust and judgment. This is Zunkiree Labs' view of the model, not a study of agency results.

## Key Takeaways

- Marketing SaaS gives your team tools to operate. GaaS gives you agents that carry out a defined task and return a result.
- Pricing moves from per seat to per outcome, such as a finished report, which can fit agency billing better than idle seats.
- Good fits are repeatable, checkable tasks: weekly reporting, keyword and competitor research, first drafts, tagging and data clean-up.
- Poor fits are brand strategy, client relationships and anything where one wrong output damages trust.
- Always keep a human approval step before anything reaches a client or goes live.

## What Does GaaS Mean for an Agency?

GaaS stands for Agentic as a Service. Where a traditional tool needs someone to log in, configure it and run it, an agent is given a goal, plans the steps and returns a finished result. For the full definition, see [what GaaS means](/resources/what-is-gaas/) and the [technical guide to GaaS infrastructure](/agentic-as-a-service/).

For an agency, the practical difference is who does the clicking. With SaaS, your team uses the reporting tool. With GaaS, the agent builds the report and your team reviews it.

## Which Agency Tasks Suit GaaS?

| Task | Fit | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly client reporting | Good | Same shape every week and easy to check against the source data |
| Keyword and competitor research | Good | Gathering and sorting, with an analyst judging the result |
| First drafts of briefs or posts | Good, with edit | Saves a blank page but needs an editor |
| Tagging, clean-up, data entry | Good | Repetitive and rule-based |
| Brand strategy and positioning | Poor | Needs judgment and client context |
| Client communication | Poor | Trust is built by people |

## How Does Pricing Change?

SaaS tools are usually priced per user per month, so the cost is the same in a quiet month. GaaS is usually priced per outcome delivered. For an agency that bills clients per deliverable, that can line up better: the cost of a report arrives with the report. Check the unit carefully, though. What counts as one outcome, and what happens if the output has to be redone?

## What Are the Risks?

- **Quality drift.** An agent can produce fluent work that is wrong. Spot-check against sources.
- **Client data.** Know what the agent can see, where it is stored and who can read it.
- **Over-promising.** Do not tell clients a task is automated if a person still signs it off.
- **Lock-in.** Keep your prompts, templates and approved examples in your own files.

## How Should an Agency Test It?

1. Pick one repeatable task, such as the weekly report for one client.
2. Run the agent alongside your normal process for a month.
3. Compare accuracy, hours saved and the number of corrections.
4. Only then extend it to a second client or task.

## The Takeaway

GaaS is a delivery model, not a shortcut. For agencies it works best on repeatable, checkable tasks, with a human approving everything that leaves the building. Measure on one task before you change how you work.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a GaaS marketing agency?

The phrase can mean a marketing agency that uses Agentic as a Service to do repeatable work with AI agents, or an agency that sells such services. In both cases, agents complete defined tasks and people review the results.

### Is GaaS the same as marketing automation?

No. Marketing automation follows rules you set in advance. A GaaS agent is given a goal and plans the steps itself, so its output needs review.

### Which marketing tasks should not be automated with agents?

Brand strategy, positioning and client relationships. They depend on judgment and trust, which an agent should support, not replace.

### How is GaaS priced for agencies?

Usually per outcome delivered, such as a finished report, instead of per user seat. Check what counts as one outcome and what happens if it has to be redone.

### Do I still need a human in the loop?

Yes. Keep a human approval step before anything is sent to a client or published.
