**In short:** Nepal had 31,194,091 broadband subscriptions at the end of September 2026, according to the Nepal Telecommunications Authority. The World Bank records Nepal's ICT service exports at US$174.3 million for 2025, while an industry body estimates IT services earned about US$1 billion in 2025. The gap exists because official statistics miss much of the sector's income. Use the official figure for what is recorded and the industry estimate for scale, and always say which one you mean.

## Key Takeaways

- Nepal had 31,194,091 broadband internet subscriptions at the end of September 2026. 88.47% of them were mobile broadband (Nepal Telecommunications Authority).
- The World Bank puts Nepal's ICT service exports at US$174.3 million in 2025, up from US$133.8 million in 2024.
- A government trade report, as reported by the Kathmandu Post, counted Rs22.33 billion of telecommunications, computer and information services exports in FY 2024/25, which is 9.36% of service exports.
- The Nepal Association for Software and IT Services (NAS-IT) estimates IT service exports crossed US$1 billion in 2025. That is an industry estimate, not an official statistic.
- Official numbers undercount the sector. The government report itself says many freelancers are paid outside the banking system, and NAS-IT says much of the income is booked as remittances.
- The World Bank records 46.32% of Nepal's population as internet users in 2024.

Where two sources disagree, both are shown. Rows marked **(industry estimate)** or **(as reported by)** are not taken straight from an official agency page, and we say so in the row.

Subscriptions are not people. The NTA counts one SIM or connection as one subscription, so its penetration figures can exceed 100% of the population. The NTA reports 103.72% for voice and 148.52% for broadband on its own basis. The World Bank and ITU figures count individuals, which is why they are lower. The ITU says some of its indicators were estimated because complete national statistics were not available, according to the Kathmandu Post.

| IT companies exporting services in the IIDS study | 106 · 2022 | [IIDS](https://iids.org.np/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/unleashing.pdf) |
| Personal remittances received | 32.3% of GDP · 2025 | [World Bank](https://api.worldbank.org/v2/country/NPL/indicator/BX.TRF.PWKR.DT.GD.ZS?format=json&per_page=5) |
| Remittance inflows | Rs1,659.41 billion, up 39.1% (US$11.55 billion, up 31.9%) · first nine months of FY 2025/26 (to mid-April 2026) | [Nepal Rastra Bank](https://www.nrb.org.np/contents/uploads/2026/05/CMEFs_Eng_Nine-Months_2082.83.pdf) |

**Why the export figures differ.** The World Bank and the government report count what passes through recorded channels. The Kathmandu Post reports that the government report itself says official statistics underestimate IT exports because many freelancers are paid outside the banking system. NAS-IT's president says most foreign currency earned by IT professionals is recorded as remittance, not as service exports. The NAS-IT estimate extends the 2022 IIDS survey with roughly 20% annual growth, according to the same article. It is a best estimate by the industry body, not an audited number. The IIDS study is a survey of 86 IT companies and 389 freelancers, supported by the World Bank's Digital Development Partnership, and covers 2022 only.

## How we collected these numbers

We collected these figures on 20 November 2026. The rules we followed:

- **Official or primary sources only.** We used Nepal Telecommunications Authority and Nepal Rastra Bank documents, World Bank data and a World Bank-supported research report. Where an official figure was only available through a news report on it, the row says "as reported by".
- **We opened every source.** A figure is in a table only if we read it on the page or document linked in its row.
- **Nothing is estimated by us.** We did not calculate, round up or extrapolate. We rounded World Bank values to one decimal place for readability.
- **Reference period in every row.** Some data is from 2022 because no newer survey exists.
- **Industry estimates are labelled.** The NAS-IT figures are marked as industry estimates.
- **Where sources disagree, we show both.** This applies to internet users and to IT exports.

What we left out: any figure we could not open, any commercial ranking or tracker, and any number whose original source a news article did not name.
