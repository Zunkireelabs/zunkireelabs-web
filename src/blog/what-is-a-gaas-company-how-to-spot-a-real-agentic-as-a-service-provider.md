**Short answer:** a GaaS (Agentic as a Service) company builds and operates autonomous AI agents and delivers them to customers as a managed service. Instead of selling software licences or user seats, it takes responsibility for the agents doing the work and is paid for completed outcomes. Zunkiree Labs describes itself as a GaaS company on this basis.

<div class="container-custom py-12 md:py-20">

## GaaS Company vs SaaS Vendor

A SaaS vendor sells a tool your team learns and operates, usually priced per user per month. A GaaS company runs agents that carry out the task for you. The vendor designs, deploys and operates the agents, so you delegate the work rather than staff the tool. For the full definition and a side-by-side table, see [What is GaaS?](/resources/what-is-gaas/) and our [Agentic as a Service overview](/agentic-as-a-service/).

## What a Real GaaS Provider Should Show You

"Agentic" is now used loosely. These are the checks that separate a provider that runs agents in production from one that has renamed a chatbot.

- **A defined outcome.** Pricing should map to something countable, such as a document processed or a lead qualified, not just "AI access".
- **Human approval gates.** For high-risk actions the agent should pause for a person. Ask which actions need approval and who approves them.
- **Audit logs.** Every action an agent takes should be recorded so you can review what happened and why.
- **Bounded access.** The agent should reach only the systems and data its job needs, and should not touch production systems without an explicit gate.
- **A pilot path.** A sensible provider will start with one workflow and measure it before you widen the scope.

Zunkiree Labs lists sandboxed execution, Human-in-the-Loop gates for high-risk actions and audit logging as part of its own GaaS infrastructure. Treat those as the kind of answers to ask any vendor for, not as proof of quality: ask to see them working on your workflow.

## Questions to Ask Before You Sign

1. Which exact outcome will I be billed for, and how is it measured?
2. What happens when the agent is wrong or unsure?
3. Who can see the logs, and how long are they kept?
4. Where does my data go, and who can access it?
5. Can I stop at one workflow and leave?
