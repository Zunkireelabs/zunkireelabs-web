**In short:** Investor commitment tracking means keeping one reliable record of how much each investor has said they will invest, how firm that is, and how much has actually arrived. The most common error in a raise is a single "total committed" number that mixes casual interest with signed commitments and received funds. Define the categories, update them in one place and reconcile to funds, and the number becomes something you can act on.

## Key Takeaways

- Separate **indicated, committed and funded** amounts, and report each on its own.
- Record **conditions,** such as subject to diligence, to terms or to timing.
- Track **changes over time,** with who changed what and why.
- **Reconcile weekly** to documents received and funds in the bank.
- Give **one owner** for the master number.
- Be ready to answer "how do we know?" for any figure.

## Define the Categories

| Category | Meaning | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Prospect | Identified, not yet engaged | Contact record |
| Interested (soft) | Informal indication of amount | Email or call note |
| Committed (hard) | Formal agreement to invest a stated amount, subject to defined conditions | Signed document or confirmation |
| Documented | Subscription paperwork complete and verified | Completed forms and checks |
| Funded | Money received | Bank confirmation |
| Declined or withdrawn | Not proceeding | Note and date |

Use your legal team's definitions. The names matter less than everyone using them the same way.

## The Record for Each Investor

- Investor identity and entity, and the relationship owner.
- Amount indicated, committed and funded, with dates.
- Conditions attached to any commitment.
- Status of paperwork and verification.
- Communications and questions.
- Documents viewed in the data room, where permitted.
- Notes on preferences and history.

## Reconciliation Routine

Weekly during the raise:

1. Compare the commitment list with **received documents.**
2. Compare documented amounts with **funds received.**
3. Investigate any difference and **record the explanation.**
4. Review **conditions** that may affect closing.
5. Update the **summary:** target, committed, funded and remaining.

Daily in the final stretch.

## Avoid Common Errors

- Counting interest as commitment.
- Letting two people edit separate copies.
- Not recording reductions or withdrawals.
- Forgetting conditions that can lapse.
- Reporting a total with no breakdown.
- Mixing currencies or entities without clear rules.

## Reporting to Your Team and Investors

- Provide an **internal dashboard** with target, committed, funded, remaining and the pipeline by stage.
- Share **investor-facing updates** only as approved, with accurate figures.
- Keep a **history** so you can reconstruct the number at any date.

## Where AI Helps

- **Flagging** stalled investors and overdue follow-ups.
- **Summarising** interactions and open questions.
- **Spotting** inconsistencies between documents and the tracker.
- **Drafting** status updates for human review.

The numbers themselves should come from records, not from a model's guess.
