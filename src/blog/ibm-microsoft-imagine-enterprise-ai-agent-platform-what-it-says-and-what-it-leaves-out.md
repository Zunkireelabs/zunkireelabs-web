**In short:** On 10 November 2026 IBM and Microsoft announced Imagine Enterprise, an agentic platform they say helps enterprises build, run and govern AI agents for industry-specific processes. It starts with financial services, insurance and retail. The announcement names one customer who is "exploring" it, gives no price and no general-availability date, and lists no compliance certifications. That is useful to know before you read the headlines.

## Key Takeaways

- Imagine Enterprise combines IBM Consulting's agent assets with Microsoft's Foundry, Copilot Studio, Entra, Purview, Defender and Azure.
- IBM says it includes more than 6,000 AI agents and assets built on IBM Consulting Advantage.
- Initial industry frameworks cover financial services, insurance and retail. Manufacturing, energy, public sector and mobility are named as possible future areas.
- Clients can have IBM Consulting deploy and operate it, co-innovate with IBM, or use self-management options.
- The release states no pricing, no timeline beyond the announcement date, no compliance certifications and no data-residency terms.

## What Was Announced?

According to the [IBM newsroom release](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-11-10-ibm-and-microsoft-announce-a-new-ai-agent-platform-designed-for-industry-specific-transformation) dated 10 November 2026, Imagine Enterprise is "designed to help enterprises build, run, and govern AI agents that help people execute industry-specific processes." It targets organisations "especially in regulated industries" that want to combine human expertise with AI agents connected to enterprise data and daily processes.

## What Is in It?

The release names two groups of components.

| Source | Named components |
|---|---|
| IBM | More than 6,000 AI agents and assets on IBM Consulting Advantage, including Process Studio, Context Studio and Advantage Core |
| Microsoft | Foundry, GitHub Copilot and Copilot Studio for coordinating agents and systems; Fabric, Microsoft 365, Entra, Purview, Defender and Azure for data, identity, governance, security and cloud |

On governance, the release says the platform is built to help organisations govern agents with "controls, observability, and AI-compliance support," with identity, security and control built into the experience. Those are design goals in the company's own words, not audit results.

## Who Is Using It?

The only customer named is SEI, a financial technology and asset management provider. The release says SEI is "looking to advance" its transformation by exploring agentic technologies with IBM Consulting and support from Microsoft. The word is exploring. The release does not say SEI has deployed Imagine Enterprise.

## What Does the Announcement Not Say?

These items do not appear on the page:

- Pricing.
- A general-availability date or roadmap.
- Specific compliance certifications such as SOC 2, ISO or HIPAA.
- Data residency or model-training data policies.

For a regulated buyer, those are the questions that decide whether a platform can be used. A press release is not the place to find them, so ask for them in writing.

## What Should a Mid-Sized Business Take From It?

A large consulting-led platform fits large enterprises that want a vendor to deploy and operate agents for them. Smaller organisations often need something narrower: one workflow, a clear list of tools the agent may use, human approval at risky steps and an audit trail. Our guide to [building an AI agent for your business tools](/blog/how-to-build-an-ai-agent-for-your-business-tools-guardrails-audit-trail/) covers that scope, and [how to test AI agents before launch](/blog/how-to-test-ai-agents-before-launch-evals-red-teaming-and-regression-suites/) covers verification. For identity and credentials, see [IBM Agent Identity](/blog/ibm-agent-identity-vault-2-2-how-to-govern-ai-agents/).

Before signing with any agent platform vendor, ask:

1. What exactly is generally available today, and what is planned?
2. Which compliance certifications apply, and can we see the reports?
3. Where is our data processed and is it used to train models?
4. Can we export our agents, prompts and logs if we leave?
5. Who is accountable when an agent takes a wrong action?
