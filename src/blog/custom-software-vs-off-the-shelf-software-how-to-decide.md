**In short:** Off-the-shelf software is a finished product you configure and pay to use. Custom software is built around your specific process and owned or controlled by you. Off-the-shelf is usually faster and cheaper to start. Custom wins when your process is a source of advantage, when no product fits without heavy workarounds, or when the cost of forcing your team into someone else's workflow keeps growing. Many businesses end up with a hybrid: buy the commodity parts, build the differentiating ones and connect them.

## Key Takeaways

- **Buy what is generic** (accounting, email, basic CRM). **Build what is distinctive** (the workflow that makes your service different).
- Off-the-shelf looks cheaper at the start. Compare **total cost over several years,** including licences per user, add-ons, workarounds and migration.
- Custom costs more upfront but can fit exactly, and you control the roadmap.
- **Integration** is often the deciding factor: a product that cannot connect to your other systems creates manual work.
- **Switching cost** applies both ways: leaving a product is hard, and so is maintaining a custom system without the people who built it.
- Decide with a **short trial or prototype** before committing either way.

## Side by Side

| | Off-the-shelf | Custom |
|---|---|---|
| Time to start | Days to weeks | Weeks to months |
| Upfront cost | Low | Higher |
| Ongoing cost | Subscription, often per user | Hosting and maintenance, no per-seat licences |
| Fit to your process | Good for common processes, workarounds for the rest | Built around your process |
| Features you do not use | Common | Only what you ask for |
| Roadmap control | Vendor decides | You decide |
| Integration | Depends on available connectors | Built to your systems |
| Ownership and lock-in | Vendor holds the platform | You can own the code (agree terms in writing) |
| Support | Vendor support | Your partner or team |
| Main risk | Poor fit, price rises, vendor changes | Overspend, delay, dependency on builders |

## When Off-the-Shelf Is the Right Answer

- The process is **standard** and many companies do it the same way.
- A product already covers **at least most** of what you need.
- You need to **start quickly** or test a process before investing.
- You lack the **budget or appetite** to maintain software.
- The vendor is **stable** and has integrations for your other tools.

## When Custom Is the Right Answer

- Your process **is** your advantage and a generic product would flatten it.
- You are **bending your team** around a tool, or maintaining spreadsheets alongside it.
- You need to **connect** several systems that no product links well.
- **Per-user licensing** grows faster than the value.
- You have **regulatory, data or security** needs that products do not meet.
- You are replacing **spreadsheets and manual steps** with something specific to your operation.

## The Hybrid Route

Most real systems mix both:

1. Keep **commodity systems** off the shelf.
2. Build a **thin custom layer** for the workflow that is unique.
3. **Integrate** through APIs so data flows without retyping.
4. Replace pieces over time as needs change.

This limits risk and cost while still fitting your process.

## Total Cost: What to Include

| Cost item | Off-the-shelf | Custom |
|---|---|---|
| Licences or build | Per user, per month | Design and development |
| Setup | Configuration, data migration | Requirements, testing |
| Customisation | Paid add-ons, consultants | Included in the build |
| Integration | Connectors, middleware | Built |
| Training | Often needed | Needed, but tailored |
| Maintenance | Included in subscription | Fixes, updates, hosting |
| Exit | Data export, migration | Handover, documentation |

Estimate each over three to five years for both options, using your own numbers.

## A Decision Checklist

1. Can a product do **80% of the job** without workarounds you would resent?
2. Is the process **core to how you win,** or routine?
3. How many **people** will use it, and how does per-seat pricing scale?
4. What must it **integrate** with?
5. Who will **maintain** it, and what happens if they leave?
6. What are the **data, security and compliance** requirements?
7. Can you run a **two to four week trial** or prototype?
8. What is the **cost of being wrong,** and how reversible is each choice?

## Avoiding the Common Mistakes

- **Building what you could buy,** out of habit or pride.
- **Buying and then customising so heavily** that you have built custom software on someone else's platform.
- **Skipping requirements,** so neither option fits.
- **Ignoring adoption.** The best system fails if teams do not use it. Involve users early.
- **No ownership terms.** Agree who owns code, data and documentation.
