**Short answer:** before an AI dental assistant handles patient information, a practice should know where the data is stored, who can access it, how long it is kept, whether it is used to train models, and what the vendor's contract says. The legal rules differ by country, so check the law that applies to your practice. This is general guidance, not legal advice.

<div class="container-custom py-12 md:py-20">

## Why Patient Data Needs Extra Care

An AI assistant that answers patient messages, books appointments or sends reminders sees names, contact details and often health information. Health data is treated as sensitive in most jurisdictions. In the United States, [HHS guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/sample-business-associate-agreement-provisions/index.html) says a covered entity must have a written contract with any business associate that handles protected health information for it; our [HIPAA checklist for US practices](/blog/ai-dental-assistant-patient-data-security-hipaa-checklist-for-us-practices/) covers that in detail. In the EU, [Article 9 of the GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/) prohibits processing health data by default unless a specific exception applies, such as provision of health care by someone bound by professional secrecy or explicit consent; the UK ICO [applies the same idea under UK GDPR](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/lawful-basis/special-category-data/what-are-the-rules-on-special-category-data/). Practices elsewhere should check their own national rules.

## The Checklist

- **Where is the data stored?** Ask for the country and the hosting provider, and whether data crosses borders.
- **Who can see it?** Ask for role-based access, and whether vendor staff can read patient conversations.
- **Is it used to train models?** Ask in writing whether your patients' data is used to train or improve any model, and how to opt out.
- **How long is it kept?** Ask for a retention period and a deletion process.
- **Is there an audit trail?** You should be able to see what the assistant said and did, and who accessed records.
- **What does the contract say?** In the US, a business associate agreement is the relevant document, and HHS's sample provisions include limits on use, safeguards, breach reporting and return or destruction of data at the end. In the EU and UK, [Article 28 of the GDPR](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-28-gdpr/) requires a written processor contract covering documented instructions, security, sub-processor approval, deletion or return of data and audit rights.
- **What happens on a breach?** Ask how and how fast the vendor notifies you.

## What the Assistant Should Not Do

A patient-facing assistant should hand off to staff for clinical questions, emergencies and anything it cannot answer confidently, rather than guessing. See our clinic guide on [what AI may and may not say](/blog/ai-patient-communication-clinic-guide-what-ai-may-and-may-not-say/).
