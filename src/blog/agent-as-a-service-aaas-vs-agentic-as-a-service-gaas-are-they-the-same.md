**In short:** Agent as a Service (AaaS) and Agentic as a Service (GaaS) usually describe the same idea: AI agents delivered as a managed service that works toward a goal. Neither has an official standard definition, and "GaaS" also means Games as a Service in the gaming industry. Always ask a vendor what is actually delivered, who operates it and how it is billed.

## Key Takeaways

- Sources use "AaaS" for AI agents as a service. Hostinger describes it as a cloud delivery model for hosted or managed AI agents.
- AlphaBOLD says GaaS "has no universally accepted acronym expansion or standardized cloud-service definition."
- In gaming, Games as a Service means earning ongoing revenue from a game over time, and Wikipedia treats cloud gaming as one form of it.
- GaaS can also be read as "governance as a service," which PYMNTS noted in its report on Nvidia's Jensen Huang.
- The label matters less than the answers to three questions: what is delivered, who operates it, and how results are billed.

## What Do GaaS, AgaaS and AaaS Mean?

These terms follow the "as a service" pattern of SaaS. In the AI agent context they point at roughly the same thing: agents that carry out work for you, delivered by a provider. [Hostinger](https://www.hostinger.com/ng/tutorials/?p=150836) calls AI agents as a service "a cloud delivery model that lets you use hosted or managed AI agents," where the provider manages some or most of the infrastructure and you define goals, permissions and connected tools.

[AlphaBOLD](https://www.alphabold.com/saas-vs-gaas/) (11 November 2026) describes GaaS as a model centred on executing defined business work, and says it has no universally accepted acronym expansion. So the spelling varies by author. We could not open the Gigabyte and FourWeekMBA pages you might see in search results (both blocked our request), so we do not rely on them here.

## Are Agent as a Service and Agentic as a Service the Same Thing?

In practice, mostly yes. Both describe agents that work across steps, use connected tools and move toward a goal. "Agentic" stresses that the agent acts with some autonomy. "Agent" names the thing delivered. Zunkiree Labs' own [GaaS page](/agentic-as-a-service/) says GaaS "stands for Agentic as a Service (sometimes written Agent as a Service)," so we treat them as the same idea.

The difference worth watching is not the name but the offer. AlphaBOLD says the distinction between SaaS and GaaS "is not whether AI agents exist, but what is delivered, who operates it, and how results are measured and billed."

## How Do the Terms Compare?

| Term | Usually means | Delivers | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| SaaS | Software as a Service | Applications your team uses | AlphaBOLD, Hostinger |
| AaaS | Agent (AI agents) as a Service | Hosted or managed AI agents | Hostinger |
| GaaS / AgaaS | Agentic as a Service (also written Agent as a Service) | Agents that execute defined business work | AlphaBOLD, Zunkiree Labs |
| GenAIaaS | Generative AI as a Service | Managed access to generative models | AlphaBOLD |
| GaaS (gaming) | Games as a Service | Games earning ongoing revenue, including cloud gaming | Wikipedia |
| GaaS (governance) | Governance as a Service | Named in PYMNTS as the technical expansion | PYMNTS |

A related term, AI as a Service (AIaaS), is described by Hostinger as hosted AI models or capabilities that applications call for output. That sits one level below agents, because you still build the workflow yourself.

## Why Does GaaS Also Mean Games?

In the video game industry, [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Games_as_a_service) describes games as a service as titles that earn ongoing revenue by delivering games or game content over time, much like software as a service. It adds that cloud gaming, where games "are run on remote servers on local devices," is one form of this model. That is why a search for "GaaS" mixes game industry pages with AI pages.

Some writers also read GaaS as "Generation as a Service." We could not verify that usage from a source we could open, so treat it as unconfirmed.

## Which Term Should a Buyer Trust?

None of them on its own. Because there is no standard definition, the acronym tells you little. Use questions instead:

1. **What is delivered?** An agent that completes a task, or a tool with an AI feature added?
2. **Who operates it?** Your staff, or the provider?
3. **How is it billed?** AlphaBOLD says GaaS pricing "might be subscription-based, consumption-based, or linked to defined outcomes." Outcome billing is common in the pitch but not guaranteed.
4. **What are the limits?** Hostinger lists recurring costs, data privacy concerns, vendor dependency, limited customisation and unpredictable behaviour as trade-offs.
