**In short:** People now ask assistants questions like "which clinic near me does root canals", "who sells apartments in Lalitpur" or "which consultancy helps with study in Australia". The assistant writes an answer and names a few providers. To be one of them, a clinic, property firm or school needs clear pages that answer those questions, facts that match everywhere it appears, and honest evidence. No one controls the answer, but these basics make you easy to find, quote and describe correctly.

## Key Takeaways

- The questions are specific and local. Pages that answer them directly are more useful to both readers and AI systems than general brand copy.
- Consistency matters: name, address, services, hours and key facts should be the same on your site and on every listing.
- Evidence beats adjectives. Qualifications, process steps, case examples and named sources are easier to cite than "best in town".
- Sensitive sectors need care. Do not make medical, legal or financial promises you cannot support.
- Google says no special optimization or markup is required for its AI features. Indexed, snippet-eligible, well-structured content is the baseline.
- Measure by asking the assistants, not only by looking at rankings.

## What Every Sector Should Publish

1. **A clear "what we do" page per service,** starting with a one or two sentence definition and who it is for.
2. **Location and contact details** in text, kept identical across the site and listings.
3. **Honest FAQs** based on real calls and messages, written in the customer's words.
4. **Pricing or price ranges where you can,** or an explanation of what affects price. Hiding all information leaves the answer to someone else.
5. **Proof:** credentials, years of experience, outcomes you can substantiate, and real case examples with permission.
6. **People:** who provides the service, with relevant qualifications.
7. **Fresh dates** on information that changes, such as fees, intake dates or availability.

## Clinics and Healthcare Providers

Questions people ask: which clinic offers a treatment, what a procedure involves, what it costs, whether the clinic accepts a particular insurer, how to book.

**Publish:**
- A page per treatment: what it is, who it suits, what happens, recovery and when to see a doctor instead.
- Practitioner profiles with registration and qualifications.
- Opening hours, location, parking and accessibility.
- How to book, and what to bring.
- Cost guidance where permitted.

**Be careful:** Avoid claims of guaranteed results. Do not publish patient details or testimonials without consent. Keep general information clearly separate from advice. ## Real Estate Firms

Questions people ask: what a neighbourhood is like, price trends, how buying works, fees and legal steps, which agencies handle a type of property.

**Publish:**
- Area guides with facts you can stand behind and a visible date.
- Buyer and seller process pages: steps, documents, timelines and typical fees.
- Listing pages with consistent, complete details.
- Team profiles and licences.
- Honest explanations of market conditions, with named sources for any figures.

**Be careful:** Out-of-date listings and prices are the most common error AI answers repeat. Remove or update sold or withdrawn properties. ## Schools, Colleges and Education Consultancies

Questions people ask: courses and entry requirements, fees, scholarships, application deadlines, visa or admission steps, outcomes.

**Publish:**
- A page per programme or destination: requirements, fees, intake dates and how to apply.
- Step-by-step process pages with realistic timelines.
- Partner institutions and accreditation, stated precisely.
- Staff qualifications and how enquiries are handled.
- Outcomes you can document, without promising admissions or visas.

**Be careful:** Admission and visa rules change. Date every page and update it. Avoid guarantees. For the evidence on AI in teaching, see [why the research behind big AI claims is weaker than it looks](/blog/ai-in-education-research-audit-why-the-meta-analyses-cannot-be-trusted-yet/), and for enquiry handling read our [guide for education consultancies](/blog/ai-for-education-consultancy-student-inquiry-automation-guide/).

## Keep the Facts Consistent Everywhere

AI systems draw on more than your site. Directories, maps, review sites, news and social profiles can all shape the answer. Make a single sheet of approved facts: legal name, address, phone, hours, services, key prices and descriptions. Use it to correct every listing you control. Our post on [where AI answers get their sources](/blog/where-ai-answers-get-their-sources/) explains why this often decides what the assistant says.

## Structured Data

Add accurate structured data where it fits, such as organisation, local business, FAQ and article markup, and make sure it matches what is visible on the page. Google says no special schema is required to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, so treat it as good housekeeping rather than a trick.

## A 30-Day Plan

1. **Week 1:** Write down the 30 questions customers ask. Check what assistants say today. See [how to measure AI search visibility](/blog/how-to-measure-ai-search-visibility-a-simple-monthly-method/).
2. **Week 2:** Fix your fact sheet and every listing.
3. **Week 3:** Publish or rewrite pages for your top five services or programmes.
4. **Week 4:** Add FAQs, dates and evidence. Re-run the checks.

## The Short Version

For clinics, property firms and schools, being named by an AI assistant comes from the same things that earn trust from people: clear answers, consistent facts, honest evidence and current information. Publish for the questions customers ask, keep every listing aligned, avoid promises you cannot keep and check regularly what the assistants say.
