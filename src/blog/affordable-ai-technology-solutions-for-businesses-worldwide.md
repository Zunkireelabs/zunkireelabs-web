## What "affordable AI" actually depends on

<p>"Affordable" has no fixed price. What an AI system costs depends on what it has to do, how much data it touches, how many people use it and how it connects to your other systems. This guide does not quote prices, because any figure without a defined scope would be a guess. It explains where the cost comes from and which choices make it easier to control.</p>

## Where the cost comes from

<ul><li><strong>Data preparation.</strong> Getting documents, records or product data into a usable state is often a large part of the work.</li><li><strong>Integration.</strong> Connecting the system to your CRM, email, databases or website.</li><li><strong>Usage.</strong> Hosted language models are typically priced by usage, measured in tokens, so cost grows with volume (see the providers' pricing pages in the sources).</li><li><strong>Hosting and monitoring.</strong> Running the system, watching quality and fixing problems after launch.</li><li><strong>Change over time.</strong> Content, tools and models change, so a system needs maintenance.</li></ul>

## Ways to keep cost under control

<ul><li><strong>Start with one narrow use case.</strong> A single well-defined workflow is cheaper to build and easier to judge than a platform for everything.</li><li><strong>Prefer retrieval over training.</strong> A retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) system answers from your own documents without training a model, which is usually simpler and cheaper to keep current. Our <a href="/blog/how-to-build-rag-pipeline/" rel="noopener">RAG pipeline guide</a> explains how it is built.</li><li><strong>Use existing applications where they fit.</strong> A pre-built application can cover common needs, with custom work only where your process is genuinely different.</li><li><strong>Match the model to the task.</strong> Not every task needs the largest model. Test a smaller one on your real examples.</li><li><strong>Measure from the first week.</strong> Track the time saved or errors avoided against what you spend, and stop what does not pay off.</li></ul>

## Questions to ask any supplier

<ul><li>What is included in the quote, and what would cause it to change?</li><li>What are the running costs after launch, and who pays for usage?</li><li>Which parts use pre-built components, and which are custom?</li><li>How will we know it works, and what happens if it does not?</li><li>Who owns the code, the data and the prompts, and can we move away later?</li></ul>

<p>A supplier who answers these plainly is easier to compare than one who offers a single headline price.</p>
