**In short:** once you have chosen an IT company in Nepal, the quality of your brief decides most of what happens next. A clear scope, small paid milestones and an agreed handover protect you more than any rate negotiation. This is practical advice drawn from how software engagements generally work, not market data about Nepal.

If you are still choosing a vendor, start with our guide to [choosing an IT company in Nepal](/it-companies-nepal/) and the [list of tech companies in Nepal](/blog/top-tech-companies-nepal-2026/).

## Key Takeaways

- Write the brief around outcomes and constraints, not a feature wish list.
- Make the first milestone small, fixed in scope and paid. It tells you more than any proposal.
- Agree who owns the code, where data lives and how handover works before work starts.
- Set one reliable daily or weekly decision window, plus written async updates.

## What a Good Brief Contains

1. **The problem and who has it.** One paragraph on the user, what they struggle with today and what success looks like.
2. **Constraints.** Budget range, deadline, existing systems it must connect to, compliance or data rules.
3. **What is out of scope.** Naming what you are not buying prevents most scope arguments later.
4. **Decision rights.** Who on your side approves work, and how fast they will respond.
5. **How you will judge it.** Acceptance criteria you can test, not adjectives like "modern" or "fast".

## Structure the Work in Milestones

Break the project into milestones that each produce something you can run or review. A common pattern is a short discovery phase, then a first working slice, then expansion. Pay per milestone rather than all up front or all at the end.

The first milestone is also a test of the relationship. Ask for a fixed scope and price for it. Watch how the team handles questions, estimates and the first review cycle, because that is a preview of the rest of the engagement.

## Communication Across Time Zones

Nepal runs at UTC+5:45, so overlap with Europe, the UK and the Middle East is workable, and US clients usually get a morning or late-evening window. You do not need full-day overlap. You need a reliable window for decisions and clear written updates so nothing stalls between calls. Our [guide to time zones with a Nepal-based team](/it-companies-nepal/) covers this in more detail.

## Agree These Four Things in Writing

- **Ownership.** Who owns the source code and intellectual property on completion.
- **Data.** Where your data is stored and processed, and who can access it.
- **Handover.** Documentation, credentials, repositories and a walkthrough at the end.
- **Termination.** What happens if delivery slips or either side wants to stop.

## Warning Signs During the Engagement

- Estimates that never change even as scope changes.
- Demos that show screens but not working behaviour.
- The people from the sales meeting disappearing after signing.
- Reluctance to give you direct access to the repository or the delivery tracker.

## The Short Version

Brief for outcomes, pay small milestones, fix the first one, and settle ownership, data and handover before the first line of code.
