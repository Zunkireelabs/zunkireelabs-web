## The short answer

**Agentic as a Service (GaaS) is a delivery model where you buy an outcome handled by AI agents, not software you have to operate yourself.** Instead of licensing a tool and doing the work through its screens, you hand over a job — route an enrolment, reconcile a billing record, schedule a shift — and autonomous agents reason over your data and act across your systems to complete it. The product you pay for is the finished work, not the dashboard. At [Zunkiree Labs](https://zunkireelabs.com/agentic-as-a-service/), an AI development company in Kathmandu building AI agents, RAG search, and voice agents for businesses worldwide, that model is already running in production across four industries rather than sitting in a slide deck.

## GaaS vs SaaS: the real shift

The easiest way to understand GaaS is to contrast it with the software model everyone already knows. Software as a Service hands you an application and expects you to drive it — your staff log in, read the screen, make the decisions, and click through every step. Agentic as a Service inverts that: the agent reads the context, makes the routine decision, and takes the action, surfacing to a human only when judgment is genuinely required. We walk through this distinction in detail in [the key differences between GaaS and SaaS](https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/understanding-the-key-differences-between-gaas-and-saas/), because the gap is less about technology and more about who does the work. With SaaS you buy capacity; with GaaS you buy completed outcomes.

## Agent as a Service vs Agentic as a Service

These two terms get used interchangeably, but they are not identical, and the difference matters when you are buying. "Agent as a Service" (AaaS) tends to describe a single agent exposed as a service — one capability you call on. "Agentic as a Service" describes a coordinated layer of agents that plan, delegate, and act across multiple systems toward a larger goal. One is a tool; the other is an operating model. If you are trying to work out which one a vendor is actually selling, our breakdown of [AaaS vs GaaS and whether they are the same](https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/agent-as-a-service-aaas-vs-agentic-as-a-service-gaas-are-they-the-same/) separates the marketing from the mechanics so you know what you are committing to.

## GaaS vs RPA: why it isn't just automation

Robotic Process Automation promised something that sounds similar — software doing repetitive work — but it does it by following rigid, pre-recorded scripts. Change a button, a field, or a workflow, and the automation breaks and someone has to rebuild it. Agentic systems reason over the situation rather than replaying a fixed path, so they adapt when reality shifts. That resilience is the core difference, and it is why agentic approaches extend to messy, judgment-heavy tasks that RPA never handled well. Our comparison of [how GaaS differs from robotic process automation](https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/gaas-vs-rpa-how-agentic-as-a-service-differs-from-robotic-process-automation/) lays out where each fits and where RPA simply runs out of road.

## What GaaS looks like in practice

Abstract definitions only go so far, so it helps to see the model doing real work. In education, agents handle enrolment routing and response for consultancies and study-abroad recruiters — the Admizz deployment runs live today with **45% faster response times**. In healthcare, the same approach replaces manual shift rostering and credential tracking for Australian providers, connecting workforce and recruitment in one system. In real estate, it secures investor data rooms and commitments tracking for capital-raise workflows. We collect more of these in [GaaS examples — what agentic as a service looks like in practice](https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/gaas-examples-what-agentic-as-a-service-looks-like-in-practice/), because production proof is what separates a real offering from a concept.

## How to spot a genuine GaaS provider

Because the term is new and fashionable, plenty of vendors have relabelled ordinary software or a thin chatbot as "agentic." The test is whether agents actually act across your systems and own outcomes, or merely suggest answers you still have to execute. Look for deployments running in production, clear ownership of the work, and infrastructure built to scale beyond a single pilot. Our guide to [spotting a real agentic as a service provider](https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/what-is-a-gaas-company-how-to-spot-a-real-agentic-as-a-service-provider/) gives you the questions to ask, and a plain-language primer on [what GaaS is](https://zunkireelabs.com/resources/what-is-gaas/) covers the foundations if the concept is new to your team.

## GaaS and the shift to service-as-software

GaaS belongs to a broader movement sometimes called "service as software" — the idea that work previously sold as billable human hours is being repackaged as software that delivers the result directly. That reframing explains why pricing, procurement, and vendor relationships look different under the agentic model, and we unpack it in [service as software and how it relates to GaaS](https://zunkireelabs.com/blog/service-as-software-what-it-means-and-how-it-relates-to-gaas/). The thread running through all of it is the same shift we opened with: you stop buying the means and start buying the outcome, with agents doing the connective work in between.
