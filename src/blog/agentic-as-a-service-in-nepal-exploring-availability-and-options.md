## Introduction to Agentic-as-a-Service

Agentic-as-a-Service (GaaS) is a cloud delivery model where autonomous AI agents are deployed as managed services to complete real business tasks — handling customer support tickets, processing documents, or running multi-step workflows — without a human operating each individual action. Rather than buying software and configuring it yourself, a business subscribes to an outcome: the agent plans the work, executes it, and hands back completed results. It's an evolution of SaaS, applied to autonomous execution instead of static tools.

## The Growing AI Market in Nepal

Nepal's tech sector has grown quickly over the past few years, with Kathmandu emerging as a hub for AI-native software companies serving both local and international clients. Local businesses — from ecommerce platforms to healthcare providers — are increasingly adopting AI tools for customer support, search, and data automation, driven by the same pressure global companies face: rising customer expectations and a need to do more with smaller teams. As global AI infrastructure costs fall and cloud-based agent platforms become easier to deploy, that adoption curve is only accelerating.

These providers deploy AI agents for tasks like automated customer support, document processing, and business analytics, giving Nepali businesses access to the same agentic automation available in more mature AI markets, without needing to build the underlying infrastructure themselves.

## Advantages of Using Agentic-as-a-Service

Businesses in Nepal adopting GaaS typically see three concrete benefits: scalability, since an agent can handle a surge in support volume or transactions without hiring and training additional staff; efficiency, since agents work continuously and complete multi-step tasks that would otherwise require coordinating several people; and cost-effectiveness, since GaaS is usually billed for outcomes delivered rather than requiring large upfront infrastructure investment.

## Challenges and Considerations

Adopting GaaS isn't friction-free. Businesses need clean, accessible data for an agent to work from, which can mean upfront investment in data organization. Integration with existing systems — CRMs, inventory platforms, communication tools — takes real engineering work, and market readiness varies: some Nepali businesses are still building the basic digital infrastructure that agentic automation depends on. Choosing a provider that offers hands-on onboarding, rather than a self-serve tool with no support, makes a meaningful difference in how smoothly that transition goes.

## Future Prospects of Agentic-as-a-Service in Nepal

As more Nepali companies digitize their operations and cloud infrastructure costs continue to fall, GaaS adoption in Nepal is likely to expand beyond early adopters in tech and ecommerce into sectors like healthcare, manufacturing, and financial services. Local providers with AI-native infrastructure are well positioned to serve this demand directly, rather than businesses having to rely solely on international platforms with less context on the local market.

## Conclusion

Agentic-as-a-Service is no longer a concept businesses in Nepal have to source from abroad — it's available locally, with providers like Zunkiree Labs already deploying autonomous agents for search, commerce, and enterprise operations. For businesses looking to scale without proportionally scaling headcount, GaaS is worth a serious look, and the earlier a business builds the data foundation to support it, the more it stands to benefit as the technology matures.
