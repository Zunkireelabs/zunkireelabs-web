## Introduction to Agentic-as-a-Service

Agentic-as-a-Service (GaaS) is a delivery model where autonomous AI agents are provided as a managed service, rather than as software tools a team has to learn and operate. Instead of paying for seats or licenses, a business delegates a task — resolving support tickets, processing documents, qualifying leads — to an agent that plans, acts, and reports on the outcome with minimal human intervention. It follows the same naming pattern as SaaS (Software-as-a-Service), but the product being delivered is a working agent rather than a piece of software someone still has to operate. For businesses evaluating it, the distinction that matters most is what they're actually paying for: SaaS is billed per seat regardless of use, while GaaS is typically billed per completed outcome.

## The Growing AI Market in Nepal

Nepal's IT and AI sector has been expanding steadily, with Kathmandu emerging as the country's main hub for AI-first companies, alongside a growing base of software development and outsourcing firms. Local businesses across retail, real estate, construction, and professional services are increasingly adopting AI tools — not just chatbots bolted onto existing systems, but infrastructure-level automation that handles scheduling, document processing, and customer communication directly. This shift is driven by the same pressures businesses everywhere face: rising customer expectations for instant service, and the need to do more with smaller teams. As more Nepali companies get exposure to what well-built AI systems can do, demand is shifting from generic AI add-ons toward providers who can build and operate agents that actually complete work, not just answer questions.

Zunkiree Labs' own client work illustrates what agentic delivery looks like in practice, even outside Nepal's borders: an AI-powered booking system that automatically matches bookings to available staff and fills cancelled slots from a waitlist, a document-processing system that classifies and routes incoming records without manual sorting, and AI-native product search that understands natural-language queries instead of requiring exact keyword matches. In each case, the agent doesn't just surface information for a human to act on — it completes the task end-to-end, with the client paying for the resulting outcome (more bookings filled, documents processed, searches converted) rather than for software access.

## Advantages of Using Agentic-as-a-Service

The main advantage is that GaaS removes the learning curve that comes with adopting new software: a business delegates a goal instead of training staff on a new tool. Pricing tied to outcomes also means the incentives are aligned — the provider only gets paid when the work actually gets done, rather than collecting a subscription regardless of usage. Scaling is elastic too: instead of hiring and training additional staff to handle a growing workload, a business can extend an existing agent's capacity, since agents don't require onboarding time the way new hires do. Because GaaS providers typically build the retrieval layer, data pipeline, and application logic together around a specific business's own data and rules, the resulting systems also tend to fit how that business actually operates, rather than forcing a workflow to bend around a generic tool.

## Challenges and Considerations

Handing a task to an autonomous agent raises legitimate questions around trust and control, which is why credible GaaS providers build in safeguards rather than treating full autonomy as the goal. High-risk actions — financial transactions, production deployments — should require a Human-in-the-Loop approval gate before an agent proceeds, and execution should happen in isolated, ephemeral environments rather than with standing access to a business's live systems. Market readiness is a real factor in Nepal specifically: some businesses are still building the basic data infrastructure an agent needs to work from, which means the first step of a GaaS engagement is often an audit of existing systems and data, not agent deployment itself. Businesses evaluating a provider should ask directly how agents are sandboxed, what triggers a human approval step, and what audit logging exists — the answers separate infrastructure-level GaaS from a chatbot with an autonomous-sounding pitch.

## Future Prospects of Agentic-as-a-Service in Nepal

As more Nepali businesses see agentic systems working in practice — through case studies, referrals, and their own early pilots — GaaS is likely to move from a novel concept to a standard delivery option alongside traditional software and outsourced development. The businesses most likely to adopt it early are the ones already running workflows that are repetitive but require judgment: scheduling, document routing, customer inquiry handling, and research synthesis are all reasonable starting points, since the outcome is measurable and the current manual cost is high. As local providers like Zunkiree Labs continue operating and refining this model with real clients, both inside Nepal and internationally, the track record needed to make GaaS a mainstream choice — rather than an experimental one — should keep building.

## Conclusion

Agentic-as-a-Service is already available in Nepal, not just as a theoretical model but as an operating one, with Zunkiree Labs running it for clients today. For businesses weighing whether to adopt it, the practical starting point isn't a wholesale platform switch — it's identifying one repetitive, judgment-heavy workflow and evaluating whether an outcome-based agent could handle it better than the current tool-plus-human-operator setup. As adoption grows and more local case studies accumulate, that evaluation should only get easier to make with confidence.
