**In short:** narrow AI does specific tasks, AGI (artificial general intelligence) would match human ability across most tasks, and superintelligence would exceed it. The terms are definitions of capability, not products you can buy today, and different people define the thresholds differently. This post explains the vocabulary. It is not a forecast.

## Key Takeaways

- **Narrow AI:** systems built or trained for particular tasks. This describes the AI tools businesses actually use today.
- **AGI:** a hypothetical system with human-level ability across most intellectual tasks. There is no single agreed test for it.
- **Superintelligence:** a hypothetical system that outperforms humans across most tasks. See [What Is Superintelligence and Why Is It Called That?](/blog/what-is-superintelligence-and-why-is-it-called-that/).
- For most businesses, the practical question is how to use today's AI safely, not when AGI arrives.

## The Three Terms

| Term | Meaning | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Narrow AI | Does specific tasks well, such as translation, search or coding help | In everyday use |
| AGI | Human-level across most intellectual tasks | Hypothetical, contested definition |
| Superintelligence | Beyond human level across most tasks | Hypothetical |

## Why Definitions Differ

There is no single threshold that everyone accepts. One research paper, [Levels of AGI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02462), proposes grading systems by both performance and generality precisely because existing definitions disagree. Researchers and companies draw the line for "general" intelligence in different places, and some argue the terms are too vague to be useful. When you read a claim that AGI has arrived or is near, check which definition is being used before comparing it to another claim.

## Why It Matters for a Business

You do not need to settle the AGI debate to make good decisions. What matters is the capability of the systems you can actually deploy, how you test them on your own work, and what oversight you keep. The debate does shape regulation and public discussion, so it is worth understanding the vocabulary. For the range of views on risk, see [the superintelligence safety debate: who wants what](/blog/superintelligence-safety-debate-who-wants-what/).

## A Practical Rule

Treat claims about future capability as separate from claims about what a tool does today. Test the tool on your own tasks, with agreed measures and visible logs.

## The Short Version

Narrow AI is here, AGI and superintelligence are definitions of capability that do not yet exist, and the thresholds are disputed. Focus on evidence about the systems you can use now.
