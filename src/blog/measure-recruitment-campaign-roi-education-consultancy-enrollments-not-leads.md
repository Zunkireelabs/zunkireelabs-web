**In short:** Most education consultancies count leads from campaigns, but leads do not pay the bills; enrollments do. To know whether a campaign works, record where every inquiry came from, follow it through each stage of the pipeline, and report cost per enrollment by source. This often reverses the picture: the cheapest lead source can be the most expensive route to a confirmed student, and a quiet referral channel can be the best.

## Key Takeaways

- **Measure to the end of the pipeline:** enrollment, not form fills.
- **Capture source on day one,** consistently, and never overwrite it.
- **Cost per enrollment** is cost per lead divided by the conversion rate through every stage.
- **Find the leak.** Conversion by stage shows whether the problem is lead quality, response speed, counselling or visas.
- **Time lags matter.** Study abroad cycles are long, so compare campaigns by cohort of inquiry date.
- **Response time** is often the cheapest lever for better results.

## Step 1: Capture Source Cleanly

At the first contact, record:

- **Channel:** social, search, referral, school visit, event, agent, walk-in.
- **Campaign:** the specific ad, event or partner, using a naming convention.
- **Destination and course interest.**
- **Date and time of the inquiry.**

Use hidden form fields and tracked links for online campaigns, and a required dropdown for phone and walk-in contacts. Reject free text that cannot be reported. If a student returns through another channel, add a touchpoint rather than replacing the original source.

## Step 2: Follow Each Cohort Through the Stages

Count how many inquiries from each source reach each stage:

| Source | Inquiries | Counselled | Applied | Offer | Visa | Enrolled |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Campaign A | n | n | n | n | n | n |
| Referral | n | n | n | n | n | n |
| Event | n | n | n | n | n | n |

Keep cohorts by inquiry month so late conversions still count to the campaign that created them.

## Step 3: Calculate Cost Per Enrollment

For each source:

- **Spend:** media, event costs, agent fees and staff time where you can allocate it.
- **Enrollments** from that source in the cohort.
- **Cost per enrollment = spend / enrollments.**

Add **conversion rates** between stages to see where students drop out. If you do not have enough volume for reliable rates, treat the numbers as directional and use longer windows.

## Step 4: Read the Funnel

| Pattern | Likely cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Many inquiries, few counselled | Slow response or poor targeting | Speed up first response, tighten the audience |
| Counselled but few apply | Wrong fit, unclear next steps, pricing | Improve counselling script and shortlist |
| Applied but few offers | Eligibility mismatch | Pre-qualify better |
| Offers but few visas | Delays, document gaps, cost | Improve follow-up and checklists |
| Visa but few enrolled | Late drop-out | Maintain contact through to arrival |

## Step 5: Make Decisions

- Shift spend toward sources with the lowest cost per enrollment, at a pace that tests whether results hold as spend increases.
- Fix **the weakest stage** before buying more leads.
- Review **response time** by source. Slower responses usually mean fewer conversions.
- Look at **quality signals** such as intake and country fit.
- Keep a small budget for **testing** new channels.

## Avoid These Mistakes

- Judging campaigns on cost per lead.
- Mixing time periods, so late enrolments are credited to the wrong month.
- Ignoring referrals and word of mouth because they are harder to track.
- Letting counsellors choose their own source labels.
- Drawing conclusions from very small numbers.

## Where AI Helps

- **Instant first responses** to new inquiries so fewer cool off. See our guide to [student inquiry automation](/blog/ai-for-education-consultancy-student-inquiry-automation-guide/).
- **Lead scoring** from past outcomes, as a guide not a rule.
- **Summaries and anomaly flags** in reports, such as a source whose conversion fell.

AI cannot fix poor tracking. The data comes first.
