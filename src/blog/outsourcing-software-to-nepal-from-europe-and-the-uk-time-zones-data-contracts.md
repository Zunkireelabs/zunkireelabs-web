**In short:** European and UK companies can work with a Nepal-based team, but three things need deciding up front: how you will overlap in working hours, where your data lives, and what the contract says about ownership and handover. This is practical guidance, not legal advice. Check data-protection obligations with your own counsel.

## Key Takeaways

- Nepal is UTC+5:45, so there is a working overlap with Europe and the UK in the European morning and early afternoon.
- You need a reliable daily decision window and written async updates, not full-day overlap.
- Decide data location and access before work starts, and take legal advice on your own obligations.
- Ownership, handover and termination belong in the contract, not in an email.

## Time Zones

Nepal is UTC+5:45 ([Time in Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Nepal)). Add that to your local offset to find the overlap. In practice the team's afternoon lines up with your morning, which suits a daily check-in, review of finished work and decisions on blockers. Agree the window explicitly and keep the rest asynchronous: written updates, a shared tracker and recorded demos mean nothing stalls between calls.

## Data and Compliance

If the work touches personal data, agree up front where it is stored and processed, who on the vendor's side can access it, and what happens to it at the end. The EU and UK each set rules for sending personal data to a country outside their own, so check the [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/international-dimension-data-protection_en) and [UK ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/international-transfers/) guidance on international transfers. When a vendor processes personal data for you, [GDPR Article 28](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-28-gdpr/) requires a written contract. For regulated work, ask the vendor how it handles access control and whether it will sign a data processing agreement. What applies to you depends on your sector and jurisdiction, so take advice on your own obligations. For the EU context, see our post on [GDPR and the EU AI Act for enterprise software in the Netherlands](/blog/enterprise-software-in-the-netherlands-gdpr-and-the-eu-ai-act/).

## Contracts

Four terms are routinely left vague and are worth writing down: who owns the source code and intellectual property on completion, where data is stored, what handover includes, and the termination terms if delivery slips. Rates and timelines get negotiated carefully. These four decide what you are left holding if the relationship ends badly.

## Running the First Project

Start with a small paid milestone at fixed scope. Judge the team on how it estimates, how clearly it writes, and how it handles the first round of review. If that goes well, extend to a larger engagement. See [software outsourcing models compared](/blog/software-outsourcing-models-dedicated-team-vs-fixed-price-vs-white-label/) for how to structure it.

## The Short Version

Agree the overlap window, the data location and the four contract terms before work begins, then test with a small milestone.
