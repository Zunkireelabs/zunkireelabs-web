**In short:** outsourcing models differ in who carries the risk and who directs the work. A dedicated team gives you capacity, a fixed-price project gives you an outcome, and a white-label build gives you an outcome under your own brand. Choosing the wrong one is a common reason outsourcing disappoints. This is practical guidance, not market data.

## Key Takeaways

- **Dedicated team:** you direct the work, so you need your own technical lead.
- **Fixed-price project:** the vendor carries delivery risk, so the scope must be written down well.
- **White-label:** the work ships under your brand, so confidentiality and client-contact rules matter most.
- Models can be combined, for example a fixed-scope first milestone followed by a dedicated team.

## The Three Models

| | Dedicated team | Fixed-price project | White-label build |
|---|---|---|---|
| You are buying | Capacity | A delivered outcome | An outcome under your brand |
| Who directs the work | You | Vendor, against agreed scope | Vendor, to your client's brief |
| Main risk | Poor direction on your side | Vague scope, change disputes | Unclear ownership and client contact |
| Fits best when | You have a backlog and technical leadership | Scope is clear and bounded | You sell the work to your own clients |

## When Each One Fits

**Dedicated team.** Choose it when the work is ongoing and changes often, and you already have someone who can set priorities and review code. Without that person, extra engineers add coordination cost instead of speed.

**Fixed-price project.** Choose it when you can describe what "done" looks like. The more you expect scope to change, the more friction a fixed price creates, because every change becomes a negotiation. Pay by milestone so you see working output early.

**White-label.** Choose it when you are an agency or software company delivering to your own clients. Agree in writing who may talk to your client, what the vendor may say about the work, and who owns the result.

## Questions to Settle Whichever You Choose

1. Who owns the source code and intellectual property on completion?
2. Where is data stored and who can access it?
3. What does handover include: repositories, documentation, credentials, a walkthrough?
4. What happens if delivery slips or either side wants to stop?
5. Who are the named people on the work, and will they stay?

## A Safe Way to Start

Whatever the model, begin with a small paid first milestone at fixed scope. It shows how the team estimates, communicates and handles review before you commit to more.
