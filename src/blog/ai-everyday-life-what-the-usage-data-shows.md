**In short:** The best recent data says AI has become an everyday tool for a large minority of people, mostly for looking things up and for work tasks, not a technology that has changed life for everyone. About half of U.S. adults use AI chatbots, yet Microsoft estimates that only about 19% of the world's working-age people use generative AI, with a wide gap between the Global North and South. Most people also say they are worried about the pace of change.

## Key Takeaways

- Pew Research Center found that about half of U.S. adults use AI chatbots, roughly one in four daily, and that searching for information is the most common use.
- Microsoft's telemetry-based estimate is that 18.8% of the world's 15-to-64-year-olds used a generative AI product in Q2 2026: 28.8% in the Global North and 16.2% in the Global South.
- Stanford's 2026 AI Index says generative AI reached 53% population adoption within three years and that adoption tracks GDP per capita. Studies use different definitions, so the numbers should not be compared directly.
- Experts and the public disagree about jobs: 73% of experts expect a positive impact on how people do their jobs, against 23% of the public.
- Use is growing faster than trust. About two-thirds of Americans say AI is advancing too quickly.


## The Evidence

This piece uses three sources, and each measures something different.

**Pew Research Center.** In [Americans and AI 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/20/2026/06/PI_2026.06.17_Americans-and-AI_REPORT.pdf) (published June 17, 2026), Pew reports on a survey of 5,119 U.S. adults conducted February 17 to 23, 2026. About half of U.S. adults say they use AI chatbots, which Pew describes as up substantially from summer 2024, and roughly one in four use them daily. Pew notes that the question wording changed in 2026, so the trend is indicative, not exact.

**Microsoft.** In [The continued state of global AI diffusion in 2026](https://blogs.microsoft.com/on-the-issues/2026/09/21/the-continued-state-of-global-ai-diffusion-in-2026/) (September 21, 2026), Microsoft estimates the share of people aged 15 to 64 who used a generative AI product, based on aggregated and anonymized Microsoft telemetry adjusted for device market share, internet penetration and population. That is a measure of use of generative AI products seen through Microsoft's data, not a survey.

**Stanford AI Index.** The [2026 AI Index Report](https://hai.stanford.edu/ai-index/2026-ai-index-report) from Stanford's Institute for Human-Centered AI states that generative AI reached 53% population adoption within three years, faster than the PC or the internet, and that adoption correlates strongly with GDP per capita.

## What the Technology Does in Daily Life

In everyday use, the main change is from search to assistants: instead of typing keywords and scanning links, people ask a chatbot a question and read an answer.

- **Looking things up.** About four in ten U.S. adults say they use chatbots to search for information, the most common use in Pew's survey.
- **Work.** 38% of employed adults say they use chatbots for tasks at work.
- **Other uses.** A quarter or less mention fun, creating or editing images or video, medical advice, or diet and fitness. About 13% use them for news, and about one in ten for emotional support.
- **Which tools.** ChatGPT is used by 44% of U.S. adults, up from 34% a year earlier and more than double its 2023 level, followed by Gemini at about a quarter of adults.

These are self-reported uses, and a person who ticks "search" may do it once a month.

## What Changed

The change is mostly one of scale and habit. Pew found that about half of U.S. adults now use chatbots, up substantially from summer 2024, and that adults under 50 are about twice as likely as those 50 and older to use ChatGPT (57% against 28%). Microsoft's estimate rose by about one percentage point in a single quarter, from Q1 to Q2 2026, to 18.8%. Stanford describes adoption as faster than that of the PC or the internet.

What has not changed much is trust. Pew found that about two-thirds of Americans think AI is advancing too quickly and only 2% too slowly. Among Americans, four in ten expect AI to have a negative impact on society over the next 20 years, far more than expect a positive one.

## Who Benefits, and Who Is Left Out

The benefits reported so far are modest and personal. Three in ten U.S. adults say chatbots help how productive they are, and 5% say they hurt it, according to Pew.

The larger story is the gap in who can use these tools at all. Microsoft puts usage at 28.8% of working-age people in the Global North and 16.2% in the Global South in Q2 2026, and says the gap is widening. It names infrastructure, connectivity and skills as barriers in the Global South. Stanford's index likewise finds that adoption correlates strongly with GDP per capita.

We did not find a Nepal-specific figure in these three reports. For a local view, see our [State of AI in Nepal 2026](/blog/state-of-ai-nepal-2026/) analysis. Reading the global numbers as a whole, a business in South Asia should expect many customers to be new to AI tools, and should plan for slow connections and for skills that vary widely.

## Limitations and Open Questions

A few cautions apply to every number above.

- **Different definitions.** Pew counts self-reported chatbot use by U.S. adults, Microsoft counts generative AI product use by people aged 15 to 64 from telemetry, and Stanford reports population adoption. Stanford's 53% and Microsoft's 18.8% are not contradictory estimates of the same thing, so do not set them side by side.
- **Mostly one country.** The most detailed usage data here is from the United States.
- **Use is not value.** Neither survey proves that AI has improved people's lives. They show that people use it and say how they feel about it.
- **Jobs are unclear.** IEEE Spectrum's summary of the AI Index says the employment data "remains mixed", with entry-level positions declining in software development and customer support. Whether AI is the cause is still being studied.
- **Experts and the public differ.** The 73% against 23% gap on job impact shows how wide the divide in expectations is.

## What Happens Next

This section is expectation, not fact. If current patterns continue, usage will probably keep rising, and the gap between rich and poor countries is the thing to watch, since Microsoft reports it widening. For businesses, the practical reading is to treat AI as a tool your customers and staff increasingly use, to be open about when you use it, and to keep a person responsible for outcomes. Our [overview of AI trends for 2026](/blog/ai-trends-and-predictions-for-2026-what-lies-ahead/) covers the broader outlook.

## The Short Version

AI is now an everyday tool for roughly half of U.S. adults, mostly for finding information and for work, but only about one in five working-age people worldwide use generative AI, and the gap between richer and poorer regions is growing. Use is rising faster than comfort with it. Read each statistic with its definition, because the surveys measure different things.
