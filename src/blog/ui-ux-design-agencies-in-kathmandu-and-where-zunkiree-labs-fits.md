## The short answer

Kathmandu has a steadily growing pool of UI/UX design studios serving web, app and product work, and "popular" usually means the ones with a visible portfolio, repeat clients and work that actually shipped. Those three signals are a sensible starting point, but they describe reputation more than capability. A screen only matters once it is connected to live data, tested under load, and placed in front of users who have a job to get done. ## What 'popular' really means for a Kathmandu design studio

Popularity among UI/UX agencies in Kathmandu tends to be a proxy for a few concrete things. A studio becomes well known when its work is visible, when clients come back, and when the designs it produces survive contact with real users instead of looking good only in a presentation. Those are reasonable signals, but they can also mislead. A polished portfolio does not guarantee that the interface was ever engineered, load-tested, or wired to live data. A beautiful case study and a dependable product are not the same artifact, and the gap between them is where budgets and timelines tend to disappear. For an education consultancy, a hospital rostering team, or a real-estate capital-raise desk, the thing that matters is whether the experience holds up when hundreds of people use it every day. So the better question is not simply which studio is popular, but which partner can take an interface from concept all the way into a dependable, working system — because that is where most design projects quietly fail. Judged that way, reputation becomes a filter rather than the final answer.

## Where UI/UX meets real AI products

Design and AI are no longer separate conversations. When a business deploys an AI CRM, a voice agent, or an orchestration layer, the interface is not decoration — it is how people trust and control the system. If users cannot read the state of an automated process, they will not rely on it, no matter how capable the underlying model is. Zunkiree Labs builds [Orca](https://zunkireelabs.com/products/orca/), which connects a business's CRM, email and marketing tools into one coordinated layer with automation workflows tailored to how that business actually operates, acting across systems instead of one at a time. An experience like that has to make a complex, multi-system process feel simple and legible to the person using it. Coordinating several tools at once is powerful, but it is also easy to turn into something opaque, and the interface is what keeps it understandable. That is a UX problem as much as an engineering one, and it is why Zunkiree Labs treats interface design as part of shipping production-grade AI infrastructure rather than a cosmetic layer added at the end. The company's pre-built AI applications are described as proven in production — not concepts, not pilots — which only works when the people operating them can navigate the workflow without friction.
