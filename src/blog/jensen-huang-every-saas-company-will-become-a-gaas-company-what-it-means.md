**In short:** At Nvidia's GTC 2026 conference, CEO Jensen Huang said "Every SaaS company will become a GaaS company," according to PYMNTS. In his use, GaaS means software that does work through AI agents instead of helping staff do it. It is a prediction, not a measured trend, and the acronym has other meanings, so buyers should ask vendors what they are really delivering.

## Key Takeaways

- PYMNTS reported the remark on 19 March 2026, from Huang's GTC 2026 keynote. The article does not give the keynote's exact date.
- The one quote PYMNTS prints is: "Every SaaS company will become a GaaS company."
- PYMNTS notes GaaS technically stands for "governance as a service," but says Huang used it for software that performs work autonomously through AI agents.
- A second page, from the agency Hobo, prints a similar line using "AgaaS" but does not link a source for it. Treat that version as unconfirmed.
- For buyers, the practical question is not the acronym. It is who does the work, and how results are measured and billed.

## What Did Jensen Huang Actually Say?

According to [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/nvidia-ceo-says-the-next-software-battle-is-over-outcomes/), published on 19 March 2026, Huang said during Nvidia's GTC 2026 keynote that "Every SaaS company will become a GaaS company." The article says the keynote took place "this week" and gives no exact date.

That is the only direct Huang quote in the PYMNTS piece. The rest of the article is the publication's own analysis of why software vendors are moving toward outcomes. We have not seen the keynote transcript, so we cannot confirm the surrounding context or the exact wording beyond what PYMNTS printed.

## What Does GaaS Mean in This Context?

PYMNTS says GaaS "technically stands for" governance as a service, but that Huang used it differently. In his framing it describes software that performs work autonomously through AI agents, rather than software that helps employees work.

A second article, from [Hobo](https://www.hobo-web.co.uk/what-is-agaas-agentic-as-a-service-explained-by-jensen-huang/) (27 April 2026), prints the line as "Every single SaaS company will become an AgaaS company, an agentic as a service company." It attributes this to a talk titled "Enterprise IT Renaissance: From SaaS to Agent-as-a-Service, 2026" but gives no date, venue or transcript link, and it offers no evidence that Huang coined the term. We could not verify that wording, so we rely on the PYMNTS version.

## Is This a Trend or Just a Slogan?

Both articles describe a direction, not a result. PYMNTS points to some signs of movement:

- It says Salesforce and Intercom are moving toward outcome-based pricing.
- It cites a Bain framing of agentic AI as three layers: systems of record, agent operating systems and outcome interfaces.
- It lists Microsoft Azure AI Foundry, Google Vertex AI Agent Builder and Amazon Bedrock Agents as early tools for building agents.

None of this proves that "every" SaaS company will change. A keynote line is a forecast. Read it as a signal about where vendors are heading, not as a guarantee.

## How Do SaaS and GaaS Differ for a Buyer?

| | SaaS | GaaS (as Huang used it) |
|---|---|---|
| What you get | Software your team operates | AI agents that do the work |
| Who does the task | Your employees | The agent, with oversight |
| What you evaluate | Features and usability | Completed work and quality |
| Typical billing | Per seat or licence | Often tied to outcomes, though not always |

The billing row is a tendency, not a rule. Independent writers note that agent services can also be priced by subscription or usage. Our own [guide to the differences between GaaS and SaaS](/blog/understanding-the-key-differences-between-gaas-and-saas/) goes through this in more detail.

## What Should Buyers Do About It?

1. **Ask what is delivered.** If a vendor says it is "GaaS" or "agentic," ask whether an agent completes a defined task or whether you still operate a tool with an AI feature added.
2. **Ask how results are measured.** Outcome pricing only works when "done" is defined. See [how to move from seat licences to outcome pricing](/blog/saas-to-gaas-how-to-move-from-seat-licences-to-outcome-pricing/).
3. **Check governance.** Because the same acronym can mean governance as a service, it is fair to ask who approves risky actions and where logs are kept.
4. **Do not rely on the label.** Our guide on [how to spot a real agentic as a service provider](/blog/what-is-a-gaas-company-how-to-spot-a-real-agentic-as-a-service-provider/) lists questions that work whatever the vendor calls itself.
5. **Pilot one workflow first.** Compare cost and quality against your current process before you widen it.
