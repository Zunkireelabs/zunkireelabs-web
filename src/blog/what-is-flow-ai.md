## Flow AI in one sentence

<p><strong>Flow</strong> is a sequence of steps or processes. <strong>AI</strong> is artificial intelligence. <strong>Flow AI</strong> is AI applied to a workflow: it often decides what should happen next and coordinates the tasks and tools involved. "Flow AI" is a descriptive term rather than a formal standard, so this article uses that definition throughout.</p>

## How it differs from a fixed workflow

<p>A traditional automated workflow follows rules written in advance: when this happens, do that. It works well when every case looks the same. Flow AI adds judgment at the points where the next step is not obvious, such as which team or tool should handle a request, what information is still missing, or when a person should step in. The steps still exist; what changes is that the choice between them can be made by an AI system working from context.</p>

## The building blocks

<ul><li><strong>Steps:</strong> the individual tasks, such as classifying a message, updating a record or sending a reply.</li><li><strong>Decisions:</strong> the points where the flow chooses between paths, which is where AI is applied.</li><li><strong>Tools:</strong> the systems the flow touches, such as a CRM, email or a calendar.</li><li><strong>Context:</strong> the information passed from step to step so that each step knows what has already happened.</li><li><strong>Oversight:</strong> a record of what the flow did and why, and a way for people to step in.</li></ul>

## How it can help

<p>When it is designed well, Flow AI can reduce the manual handoffs between tools, so work that starts in one system is carried through in the others. It can make follow-through more consistent, because every case goes through the same decision points. It can also leave a trail of what happened, which makes the process easier to review, and it can free people to spend their time on the exceptions that need judgment. How much of this you get depends on the workflow, the quality of the data and the care taken in the design; none of it is automatic.</p>

## Where it applies

<p>These are illustrations of the idea, not descriptions of results:</p>

<ul><li><strong>Inquiry handling:</strong> a message arrives, is classified, routed to the right place, and followed up in the right tool.</li><li><strong>Scheduling:</strong> a request is understood and turned into a booking in the calendar system.</li><li><strong>Cross-tool work:</strong> an action in a CRM leads to the matching steps in email and marketing tools without someone updating each one by hand.</li></ul>

## What to check before adopting it

<ul><li>Which decisions does the AI make, and which stay with people?</li><li>What happens when the AI is uncertain? Does it ask, escalate or guess?</li><li>Can you see what the flow did and why, step by step?</li><li>Which systems and data can it reach, and who controls that access?</li><li>Does it work with the tools you already run, or does it require replacing them?</li></ul>
