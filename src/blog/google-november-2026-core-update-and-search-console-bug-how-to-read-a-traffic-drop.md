**In short:** Google began rolling out its November 2026 core update on 10 November 2026, and its status dashboard still listed it as active on 19 November. During the same days, 9 to 11 November, Google documented a logging error that lowered impressions in the Search Console Generative AI performance report. If your impressions or clicks fell this month, two different things may be overlapping. Google's advice is to wait until the rollout finishes, then wait another full week before analysing.

## Key Takeaways

- Google's Search Status Dashboard records the November 2026 core update as started on 10 November 2026 at 10:06 Pacific time, applying "globally and to all languages."
- Google says the rollout "may take up to 2 weeks to complete." As of the dashboard's 19 November snapshot, no completion time was shown.
- Google's Data anomalies page lists a logging error for 9 to 11 November 2026 that decreased impressions in the Generative AI performance report. Google says it affects data logging only.
- Google recommends waiting at least a full week after a core update completes before analysing your site in Search Console.
- After a small drop, Google advises against changing content that already performs well. Deleting content is described as a last resort.

## What Did Google Announce?

Google's [Search Status Dashboard](https://status.search.google.com/) lists the "November 2026 core update" under the Ranking product. The incident began on 10 November 2026 at 10:06 Pacific time. Google's entry says the update was released, applies globally and to all languages, and that the rollout may take up to two weeks. Counting from the start date, that points to roughly 24 November, but that date is arithmetic, not something Google has stated. The dashboard showed no end time on 19 November.

## What Was the Search Console Bug?

Google's [Data anomalies page](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/6211453?hl=en) lists "November 9 - November 11 (Generative AI in Search)". It says a logging error caused a decrease in impressions on the Generative AI performance report in Search, and that the issue affects data logging only. [Search Engine Roundtable's report](https://www.seroundtable.com/google-generative-ai-performance-report-data-bug-42527.html) of 12 November describes it as a reporting bug with no effect on performance in Google Search. That wording is the publication's, not Google's.

Two points are worth stating plainly. The anomaly entry names the Generative AI report, not every Search Console report. And at the time of reading, the page showed no restoration note for this entry, which is a fact about the page, not proof that the problem persists.

## How Do You Tell a Reporting Dip From a Ranking Loss?

The bug and the update started within a day of each other, so a single chart cannot separate them. Use a few checks that can.

1. **Check which report you are reading.** If the drop is only in the Generative AI performance report and only on 9 to 11 November, it matches the documented logging error.
2. **Compare with a source Google does not control.** Your own analytics sessions from organic search show whether visitors actually fell. A drop in Search Console impressions with flat analytics sessions points to measurement, not rankings.
3. **Look at position, not just clicks.** A core update changes rankings. If average position for your main queries slipped after 10 November, that is a ranking signal. If position is stable and only impressions moved in the bug window, it is not.
4. **Look at pages, not the whole site.** Core updates tend to move some pages and leave others. Split the Performance report by page and by query group.
5. **Do not judge yet.** Google says to confirm the rollout has finished, then wait at least a full week before analysing, comparing the week after completion with the week before the rollout began.

## What Does Google Say to Do After a Core Update?

According to [Google's core update guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/updates/core-updates):

- Confirm the update has finished rolling out on the Search Status Dashboard.
- Wait at least a full week after completion before analysing Search Console data.
- After a small drop, avoid changing content that is already performing well.
- After a large, sustained drop, read Google's helpful-content self-assessment and assess the site as a whole and its most affected pages.
- Avoid quick fixes. Consider meaningful content improvements, and treat removing content as a last resort.
- Expect recovery to take time. Google says it can take several months, and in some cases may wait for the next core update.

## What Should a Business Do This Week?

Do not rewrite pages in a panic while the rollout is still active. Instead, record a baseline now: the date range, the queries and pages that moved, and your analytics sessions for the same days. When the dashboard shows the update as complete, wait a week, then repeat the comparison. If the drop is real and concentrated in a group of pages, that group is your improvement list.
