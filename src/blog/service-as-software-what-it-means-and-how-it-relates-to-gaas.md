**In short:** "Service as software" is the idea that AI agents let a company sell a finished result, not a tool the customer has to operate. Agentic as a Service (GaaS) is one name for the delivery model that follows from it. Neither label is a standard, so the useful question for a buyer is simple: who is responsible for the result?

## Key Takeaways

- In SaaS the customer uses the tool and stays responsible for the outcome. In service as software, the seller takes responsibility for the outcome.
- Foundation Capital's example: an AI accountant offering tax services instead of selling QuickBooks.
- Foundation Capital calls the resulting market $4.6 trillion. That is the author's own estimate, not an independent forecast.
- GaaS is a nonstandard label with "no universally accepted acronym expansion," according to AlphaBOLD.
- Sources disagree on whether SaaS is displaced. Foundation Capital says the shift "does not always spell the end for the SaaS model."

## What Does "Service as Software" Mean?

Foundation Capital's article describes a shift from software as a service to service as software, which it also calls "software as autonomous service." In its framing, a SaaS company sells access to a platform and the customer must use it to reach the desired result. In service as software, the company sells the result itself. As the article puts it: "In the services business, responsibility for achieving the desired outcome sits with the company selling the service."

The reason it is being discussed now, according to the article, is that agents powered by large language models can interpret content, set priorities and direct tasks. Software then changes how a job is done, not only how the workflow is organised.

## How Does It Relate to Agentic as a Service?

They describe the same direction from two sides. Service as software is the business idea: sell the work, not the tool. GaaS is a label for the delivery model, where an agent or platform carries out specified tasks. AlphaBOLD describes GaaS as an arrangement where a provider or platform carries out specified tasks, such as retrieving a customer record, verifying an order, taking authorised steps and escalating exceptions. It also warns that agent-building tools, such as Microsoft Copilot Studio, are not automatically outcome-priced GaaS contracts, and that GaaS is distinct from Generative AI as a Service, which is managed access to models.

Zunkiree Labs describes GaaS on its [Agentic as a Service page](/agentic-as-a-service/) as a model where autonomous agents are delivered as a managed service and paid for by outcome. For a beginner's definition, see [what is GaaS](/resources/what-is-gaas/).

## How Does Pricing Change?

Foundation Capital says outcome-based pricing aligns cost with value and removes the vendor's incentive to limit usage. Its example is a sales tool charging per qualified opportunity or signed customer. L.E.K. Consulting reached a similar view in June 2025 ("Rather than buying licenses, they'll pay for outcomes") but did not say how outcomes would be measured.

| | SaaS | Service as software |
|---|---|---|
| What is sold | Access to a tool | The finished result |
| Who does the work | The customer's staff | An AI agent, with human oversight chosen per function |
| Who is accountable for the result | The customer | The seller |
| Typical pricing | Per seat or tier | Often tied to outcomes, though hybrids exist |

The last row is a general reading of the sources, not a rule. See our guide to [how outcome-based pricing works and its risks](/blog/outcome-based-pricing-for-ai-agents-per-seat-per-task-per-outcome/).

## What Is Still Unproven?

- **The market size.** The $4.6 trillion figure is Foundation Capital's own estimate, combining $2.3 trillion in salaries with $2.3 trillion in outsourced IT and business process services (the latter attributed to Gartner). It describes spend that could be addressed, not revenue anyone has earned.
- **Examples are mostly start-ups.** The article names companies such as Wizia, AirMDR, ConverzAI, Docket and Tennr. They illustrate the idea, but the page gives no verified results for them.
- **Billing is not value.** AlphaBOLD notes that "Outcome billing alone is not proof of business value."
- **Governance moves.** L.E.K. warns that agentic systems must stay auditable, secure and aligned with regulations, and that regulated industries may resist autonomous decisions.
- **SaaS may adapt.** L.E.K.: "Agentic AI doesn't necessarily spell the end of SaaS, but it certainly marks the end of SaaS as we know it."

## What Should a Business Do With the Idea?

1. Pick one workflow where the result is easy to count.
2. Ask any vendor who is accountable when the result is wrong.
3. Check how the vendor defines and measures the outcome. See [what makes a real GaaS provider](/blog/what-is-a-gaas-company-how-to-spot-a-real-agentic-as-a-service-provider/).
4. Keep human review where mistakes are costly.
5. Compare the full cost against your current tools and staff.
