**In short:** A capital raise is the process by which a commercial real estate sponsor assembles equity from investors for a deal: preparing the offering, introducing it to investors, handling questions, collecting commitments, completing onboarding and closing. Most sponsors run it across email, spreadsheets and file-sharing links, which gets hard as investor numbers grow. A single workflow with a clear status for each investor and a controlled place for documents makes the process faster and easier to defend.

## Key Takeaways

- Treat the raise as a **pipeline of investors** moving through defined stages, not a series of emails.
- Keep **one source of truth** for who has been contacted, who has committed and how much.
- Distinguish **indicated interest, formal commitment and funded** amounts. They are not the same.
- Share sensitive documents through a **controlled data room** with access rights and logs.
- Plan **closing tasks** early: documents, verification, funding and reporting.
- **Rules vary** by jurisdiction and offering type. Work with qualified legal and compliance advisers, and make the system follow their process.

## The Stages of a Raise

| Stage | What happens | What to track |
|---|---|---|
| Preparation | Deal terms, structure and materials prepared | Offering details, documents, approvals |
| Investor targeting | List of prospective investors prepared | Relationship, history, preferences |
| Outreach | Offering introduced | Date, channel, owner, response |
| Diligence and Q&A | Investors review and ask questions | Questions, answers, documents viewed |
| Commitment | Investors indicate or commit amounts | Amount, status, conditions, date |
| Onboarding | Subscription paperwork and verification | Documents received, checks completed |
| Funding and closing | Funds received, deal closes | Funded amounts, closing date, confirmations |
| Post-close | Reporting and investor communication | Distributions, updates, documents |

Your legal process may add, remove or reorder stages. Configure the workflow to match it.

## Structure the Offering Record

Hold in one place:

- Property or portfolio, business plan and key terms.
- Target raise, minimum and maximum amounts, and timeline.
- Share classes or tranches where applicable.
- Offering documents and their versions.
- Approved messaging and disclosures.
- Status: draft, open, closing, closed.

When terms change, update the record and notify affected parties through a controlled process.

## Manage Investor Communication

- **Segment investors** by relationship, history and interest.
- **Log every interaction** against the investor and the offering.
- **Answer questions once,** and share the answer with others where appropriate and permitted.
- **Send updates** at set points, such as time remaining and key developments.
- **Avoid statements outside approved materials,** and have compliance review communications as required.

## Why Spreadsheets Break Down

- Different versions of the commitment list circulate.
- Nobody can say who has the latest signed document.
- Follow-ups are missed.
- Investor questions are answered inconsistently.
- Reconciling commitments to funds received is manual.
- There is no clear record for an audit or investor query.

## Where AI Helps

- **Drafting** investor updates and answers to common questions from approved material, for review.
- **Summarising** investor interactions and flagging stalled conversations.
- **Extracting** key fields from subscription documents for staff to verify.
- **Reminding** the team about follow-ups and closing tasks.

Do not let AI make statements about returns or terms that are not in approved materials. Keep compliance review in the loop.

## Closing Checklist

1. Total commitments meet the minimum and are within the maximum.
2. Subscription documents complete and signed for each investor.
3. Required verification complete.
4. Funds received and reconciled to commitments.
5. Allocation confirmed.
6. Closing documents and confirmations issued.
7. Post-close reporting set up.
